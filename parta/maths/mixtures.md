# Mixture and Alligation – Complete Notes

## 1. What Is a Mixture?

A **mixture** is formed when two or more different substances are mixed together.

**Example:**
- Milk = 50 litres
- Water = 30 litres

Total mixture = 50 + 30 = **80 litres**

### Important formulas

- Total mixture = Quantity of substance 1 + Quantity of substance 2
- Quantity of a component = Its ratio part × Value of 1 ratio part
- Value of 1 ratio part = Total quantity ÷ Total ratio parts

---

## 2. Understanding Ratios in Mixtures

A ratio tells us the relative quantities of two substances.

**Example 1:** Milk = 50 litres and water = 30 litres.

Milk : Water = 50 : 30 = **5 : 3**

**Example 2:** Milk = 50 litres and water = 32 litres.

Milk : Water = 50 : 32 = **25 : 16**

### Rule: Adding a substance to a mixture

Suppose:
- Milk = 50 litres
- Water = 45 litres
- Additional water = 20 litres

New water quantity = 45 + 20 = 65 litres.

New ratio:

50 : 65 = **10 : 13**

**Remember:** Add the new quantity to the correct component before simplifying the ratio.

### Finding a missing quantity using a ratio

If Milk : Water = 5 : 7 and milk is 20 litres:

1 ratio part = 20 ÷ 5 = 4 litres.

Water = 7 × 4 = **28 litres**.

### Finding total mixture

If Milk : Water = 7 : 5 and water = 15 litres:

1 ratio part = 15 ÷ 5 = 3 litres.

Total mixture = (7 + 5) × 3 = **36 litres**.

### Finding individual quantities from total mixture

If Milk : Water = 5 : 3 and total mixture = 40 litres:

Total ratio parts = 5 + 3 = 8.

1 ratio part = 40 ÷ 8 = 5 litres.

- Milk = 5 × 5 = **25 litres**
- Water = 3 × 5 = **15 litres**

### Finding quantities when the difference is given

If Milk : Water = 10 : 7 and the difference between them is 15 litres:

Difference in ratio parts = 10 − 7 = 3.

1 ratio part = 15 ÷ 3 = 5 litres.

- Milk = 10 × 5 = **50 litres**
- Water = 7 × 5 = **35 litres**

**Shortcut:** When the difference is given, divide the actual difference by the difference between the ratio parts.

---

## 3. Adding Milk or Water: The Same-Component Rule

This is one of the most important concepts in mixture questions.

### Case A: Water is added

When water is added:
- Milk quantity remains unchanged.
- Water quantity increases.

**Example:**

Initial milk : water = 2 : 3.

After adding 10 litres of water, the new ratio becomes 2 : 5.

The milk ratio part is unchanged, so we can compare the two ratios directly.

- Old ratio = 2 : 3
- New ratio = 2 : 5
- Increase in water ratio parts = 5 − 3 = 2

These 2 parts represent 10 litres.

1 ratio part = 10 ÷ 2 = 5 litres.

**Initial mixture:**
- Milk = 2 × 5 = 10 litres
- Water = 3 × 5 = 15 litres

**New mixture:**
- Milk = 2 × 5 = 10 litres
- Water = 5 × 5 = 25 litres

### Case B: The unchanged component has different ratio parts

Sometimes the milk ratio is not the same in the old and new ratios.

**Example:**

- Old milk : water = 8 : 3
- 21 litres of water is added.
- New milk : water = 4 : 5

Because water is added, milk must remain unchanged.

Make the milk parts equal:

- Old ratio = 8 : 3
- New ratio = 4 : 5 = 8 : 10 (multiply both parts by 2)

Now compare the water parts:

Increase = 10 − 3 = 7 parts.

7 parts = 21 litres.

1 part = 3 litres.

**Initial quantities:**
- Milk = 8 × 3 = 24 litres
- Water = 3 × 3 = 9 litres

**New quantities:**
- Milk = 8 × 3 = 24 litres
- Water = 10 × 3 = 30 litres

### Case C: Water is added, but the milk ratios are 6 and 5

**Example:**

- Old milk : water = 6 : 5
- 66 litres of water is added.
- New milk : water = 5 : 6

Make the milk ratio parts equal using the LCM of 6 and 5:

- Old ratio = 30 : 25
- New ratio = 30 : 36

Increase in water ratio parts = 36 − 25 = 11.

11 parts = 66 litres.

1 part = 6 litres.

**Initial quantities:**
- Milk = 30 × 6 = 180 litres
- Water = 25 × 6 = 150 litres

**New quantities:**
- Milk = 30 × 6 = 180 litres
- Water = 36 × 6 = 216 litres

### If milk is added instead

The same rule works in reverse:

**When milk is added, water remains unchanged.**

Make the water ratio parts equal, then compare the increase in milk parts.

---

## 4. Removing Mixture

When a uniform mixture is removed from a container, the ratio of its components does not change.

For example, if a mixture contains milk and water in the ratio 2 : 3, removing 10 litres of that mixture leaves the remaining mixture in the ratio **2 : 3**.

The removed mixture also has milk and water in the ratio **2 : 3**.

### Solved example

A container initially holds 80 litres of milk and water in the ratio 2 : 3. If 50 litres of mixture is removed, find the quantities removed and remaining.

**Step 1: Initial quantities**

Total ratio parts = 2 + 3 = 5.

1 part = 80 ÷ 5 = 16 litres.

- Milk = 2 × 16 = 32 litres
- Water = 3 × 16 = 48 litres

**Step 2: Remaining quantity**

80 − 50 = 30 litres.

The remaining ratio is still 2 : 3.

1 part = 30 ÷ 5 = 6 litres.

- Remaining milk = 2 × 6 = 12 litres
- Remaining water = 3 × 6 = 18 litres

**Step 3: Quantities removed**

- Milk removed = 32 − 12 = 20 litres
- Water removed = 48 − 18 = 30 litres

Check: 20 + 30 = 50 litres.

The removed mixture is also in the ratio 20 : 30 = **2 : 3**.

### Important rules

- Removing a uniform mixture does not change the ratio of its components.
- Adding a new substance can change the ratio.
- The removed mixture has the same ratio as the mixture before removal.

---

## 5. Removing Mixture and Replacing It with Water

These questions involve two operations:

1. Remove some mixture.
2. Add the same quantity of water.

The total volume returns to its original amount, but the amount of milk decreases.

### Example

A container has 90 litres of pure milk. Remove 18 litres and replace it with 18 litres of water.

**Step 1: Remove 18 litres**

Milk remaining = 90 − 18 = 72 litres.

**Step 2: Add 18 litres of water**

- Milk = 72 litres
- Water = 18 litres
- Total = 90 litres

New milk : water ratio = 72 : 18 = **4 : 1**

### Repeated replacement formula

If:
- Initial mixture quantity = \(V\)
- Quantity removed and replaced each time = \(x\)
- Number of replacement operations = \(n\)

Then the quantity of the original substance remaining is:

\[
\text{Original substance remaining}
=V\left(1-\frac{x}{V}\right)^n
\]

For pure milk, this formula gives the milk remaining after repeated replacement with water.

**Important:** If a question says the operation is repeated *two more times*, count the first operation as well. That means three operations in total.

### Solved example: Two replacement operations

A container contains 90 litres of pure milk. Each time, 18 litres are removed and replaced with water. The process happens twice in total. Find the milk remaining.

Fraction of milk remaining after one operation:

\[
\frac{90-18}{90}=\frac{72}{90}=\frac45
\]

After two operations:

\[
90\times\left(\frac45\right)^2
=90\times\frac{16}{25}
=\boxed{57.6\text{ litres}}
\]

Milk remaining = **57.6 litres**.

Water in the final mixture = 90 − 57.6 = **32.4 litres**.

### Shortcut to remember

\[
\boxed{\text{Remaining original substance}
=V\left(\frac{V-x}{V}\right)^n}
\]

Use this when the same quantity is removed and replaced repeatedly, and the container's total volume remains constant.

---

## 6. Variable-Based Mixture Questions

Some questions use an unknown quantity such as \(x\) instead of a fixed number.

### General method

1. Write the initial quantities or ratio.
2. Subtract the quantity removed, if any.
3. Add the new substance.
4. Form the new ratio.
5. Use the given ratio or condition to form an equation.
6. Solve for \(x\).

**Important:** Do not assume that the total mixture changes when the same quantity is removed and replaced. In that situation, the total volume stays the same.

# PART 2: ALLIGATION

## 7. What Is Alligation?

Alligation is a method used to find the ratio in which two items with different values must be mixed to obtain a mixture with a specified average value.

It is an **approach**, not a formula limited to milk and water.

It can be used in questions involving:
- Different prices
- Different concentrations
- Different profit percentages
- Average salaries
- Population growth rates
- Different speeds
- Interest rates

### Basic condition

The target average must lie between the two values being mixed.

For example, if the two values are 4 and 10, the target average can be 6.

---

## 8. The Alligation Rule

Suppose two items have values:

- Lower value = \(L\)
- Higher value = \(H\)
- Required average = \(M\)

The ratio of quantities to mix is:

\[
\boxed{\text{Quantity at }L:\text{Quantity at }H
=(H-M):(M-L)}
\]

In words:

- Subtract the average from the higher value.
- Subtract the lower value from the average.
- Write the differences crosswise.

### Example

Mix items valued at 4 and 10 to obtain an average value of 6.

Higher value = 10.

Lower value = 4.

Average = 6.

Differences:

- 10 − 6 = 4
- 6 − 4 = 2

Therefore:

Quantity of item valued at 4 : Quantity of item valued at 10

= 4 : 2

= **2 : 1**

So, mix the cheaper item and the costlier item in the ratio **2 : 1**.

### Alligation diagram

```text
Lower value                 Higher value
     4                           10
          \                   /
              Average
                 6

Higher − Average = 10 − 6 = 4
Average − Lower  = 6 − 4  = 2

Quantity ratio = 4 : 2 = 2 : 1
```

**Remember:** The differences are written crosswise, not in the same order as the original values.

---

## 9. How to Identify What the Alligation Ratio Represents

The ratio from alligation does not always represent the same thing. It depends on the quantity being averaged.

A key rule from the lecture is to look at the denominator of the relevant rate or average.

| Given value | What the alligation ratio usually represents |
|---|---|
| Cost price per kg | Quantities or weights |
| Average salary | Number of employees |
| Speed | Time spent at each speed |
| Discount percentage | Corresponding marked prices when the overall discount is weighted by marked price |
| Profit/loss percentage | Often cost-price proportions when the question gives actual money values |
| Concentration or percentage of a component | Quantities of the mixtures being combined |

**Always check the wording.** Identify the denominator or weighting quantity before interpreting the ratio.

For example:

- Average salary = total salary ÷ number of employees. The ratio represents employee counts.
- Speed = distance ÷ time. The ratio from alligation on speed represents time, not distance.
- Price per kg = total cost ÷ quantity. The ratio represents quantities or weights.

---

## 10. Alligation with Percentages

Alligation can be used when two groups have different percentage changes and an overall percentage change is given.

### Solved example: Population growth

A town has 240,000 people. The male population increases by 7%, and the female population increases by 10%. The total population increases by 9.5%. Find the original number of males.

Values:

- Males: 7%
- Females: 10%
- Overall increase: 9.5%

Using alligation:

- 10 − 9.5 = 0.5
- 9.5 − 7 = 2.5

Male : Female

= 0.5 : 2.5

= **1 : 5**

Total parts = 1 + 5 = 6.

1 part = 240,000 ÷ 6 = 40,000.

Original male population = **40,000**.

Original female population = 200,000.

---

## 11. Alligation with Mixtures of Different Concentrations

### Example: Alcohol and water

Two containers have alcohol and water in these ratios:

- Container A: Alcohol : Water = 3 : 1
- Container B: Alcohol : Water = 3 : 5

Find the ratio in which they should be mixed to obtain a final alcohol : water ratio of 5 : 3.

**Step 1: Convert alcohol quantities into fractions.**

Container A:

\[
\frac{\text{Alcohol}}{\text{Total}}
=\frac34
\]

Container B:

\[
\frac{\text{Alcohol}}{\text{Total}}
=\frac38
\]

Required mixture:

\[
\frac{\text{Alcohol}}{\text{Total}}
=\frac58
\]

**Step 2: Make denominators equal.**

- Container A: \(3/4=6/8\)
- Container B: \(3/8\)
- Required mixture: \(5/8\)

Now use alligation with values 6, 3, and 5.

- 6 − 5 = 1
- 5 − 3 = 2

Quantity ratio:

Container A : Container B = 2 : 1.

**Answer: 2 : 1**

You can use either alcohol or water fractions. The answer remains the same if the calculations are correct.

---

## 12. Alligation with Average Salary

### Example

A company has 700 employees.

- Average salary of males = ₹4,200
- Average salary of females = ₹4,100
- Average salary of all employees = ₹4,190

Find the number of female employees.

Using alligation:

- 4,200 − 4,190 = 10
- 4,190 − 4,100 = 90

Male : Female

= 90 : 10

= **9 : 1**

Total ratio parts = 10.

1 part = 700 ÷ 10 = 70.

Number of female employees = **70**.

Number of male employees = 630.

---

## 13. Alligation with Speed

### Example

A person travels at 20 km/h for 30 minutes and at \(x\) km/h for 45 minutes. The average speed for the whole journey is 14 km/h. Find \(x\).

Since the average speed is 14 km/h, the other speed must be below 14 km/h.

Alligation on speed gives the ratio of **times**:

\[
(14-x):(20-14)=30:45
\]

Simplify the time ratio:

\[
30:45=2:3
\]

Therefore:

\[
(14-x):6=2:3
\]

\[
\frac{14-x}{6}=\frac23
\]

\[
3(14-x)=12
\]

\[
42-3x=12
\]

\[
3x=30
\]

\[
\boxed{x=10\text{ km/h}}
\]

**Important:** Alligation on speed gives the time ratio because speed is distance divided by time.

---

## 14. Alligation with Interest Rates

### Example

₹80,000 is invested in two banks.

- Bank A pays 9.5% interest.
- Bank B pays 5.5% interest.
- The total interest earned in one year is ₹6,400.

Find the amount invested in Bank A.

First calculate the overall interest rate:

\[
\frac{6400}{80000}\times100=8\%
\]

Now use alligation:

- 9.5 − 8 = 1.5
- 8 − 5.5 = 2.5

Bank A : Bank B

= 2.5 : 1.5

= **5 : 3**

Total parts = 8.

1 part = ₹80,000 ÷ 8 = ₹10,000.

Amount in Bank A = 5 × ₹10,000 = **₹50,000**.

Amount in Bank B = 3 × ₹10,000 = ₹30,000.

## 15. Alligation with Cost Price and Profit Percentage

When two items have different cost prices or profit percentages, first identify the correct average value.

### Example: Mixing two varieties of rice

- Rice A costs ₹60 per kg.
- Rice B costs ₹64 per kg.
- The mixture is sold at ₹68.20 per kg at a profit of 10%.

Find the ratio in which the two rice varieties are mixed.

**Step 1: Find the cost price of the mixture.**

Selling price is 110% of cost price.

\[
\text{Mixture CP}=\frac{68.20\times100}{110}
\]

\[
\text{Mixture CP}=\text{₹}62
\]

**Step 2: Apply alligation.**

- Higher CP = ₹64
- Lower CP = ₹60
- Average CP = ₹62

Differences:

- 64 − 62 = 2
- 62 − 60 = 2

Therefore, quantity ratio:

Rice A : Rice B = 2 : 2 = **1 : 1**

Mix equal quantities of the two varieties.

---

## 16. Alligation with Profit and Loss Percentages

The same alligation approach can be used when the average profit percentage is known.

Suppose:
- Item A gives a 4% profit.
- Item B gives a 7% profit.
- The overall profit is 6%.

If the profit percentages are weighted by cost price, apply alligation:

- 7 − 6 = 1
- 6 − 4 = 2

The cost-price ratio is:

Item A : Item B = **1 : 2**

**Important:** This is a cost-price ratio when the question is based on the actual cost prices. Read the question carefully before treating the ratio as a quantity ratio.

---

## 17. Alligation with Income, Expenditure and Savings

Remember:

\[
\boxed{\text{Income}=\text{Expenditure}+\text{Savings}}
\]

### Example

- Income decreases by 5%.
- Expenditure decreases by 8%.
- Savings increase by 4%.

Find the ratio of income to expenditure.

Represent the percentage changes:

- Income = −5%
- Expenditure = −8%
- Savings = +4%

Using the income identity:

\[
0.95I=0.92E+1.04S
\]

Also:

\[
I=E+S
\]

Substitute:

\[
0.95(E+S)=0.92E+1.04S
\]

\[
0.03E=0.09S
\]

Therefore:

\[
E:S=3:1
\]

Income = Expenditure + Savings.

So:

\[
I=3+1=4\text{ parts}
\]

Thus:

\[
\boxed{\text{Income}:\text{Expenditure}=4:3}
\]

---

## 18. Alligation with Alloys

An alloy is a mixture of metals.

### Example

Alloy A contains copper and zinc in the ratio 3 : 4.

Alloy B contains copper and zinc in the ratio 5 : 9.

Suppose 2 units of Alloy A and 3 units of Alloy B are mixed. Find the final copper : zinc ratio.

**Step 1: Find the fractions in each alloy.**

Alloy A:
- Copper = \(3/7\)
- Zinc = \(4/7\)

Alloy B:
- Copper = \(5/14\)
- Zinc = \(9/14\)

**Step 2: Calculate copper in the final mixture.**

\[
2\times\frac37+3\times\frac5{14}
\]

\[
=\frac{12}{14}+\frac{15}{14}
=\frac{27}{14}
\]

**Step 3: Calculate zinc in the final mixture.**

\[
2\times\frac47+3\times\frac9{14}
\]

\[
=\frac{16}{14}+\frac{27}{14}
=\frac{43}{14}
\]

Therefore:

\[
\text{Copper}:\text{Zinc}
=\frac{27}{14}:\frac{43}{14}
\]

Cancel the common denominator.

**Final ratio = 27 : 43**

## 19. Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Adding the wrong component:** If water is added, increase water, not milk.
2. **Changing the unchanged component:** When water is added, milk must remain the same. When milk is added, water must remain the same.
3. **Forgetting to simplify ratios:** For example, 50 : 30 becomes 5 : 3.
4. **Using the old ratio for the new mixture:** After adding a substance, calculate the new ratio.
5. **Changing the ratio after removing a uniform mixture:** Removing a uniform mixture leaves the ratio unchanged.
6. **Forgetting replacement operations:** Count the total number of times the operation occurs.
7. **Applying alligation when the average is outside the two values:** The target average must lie between the two values for ordinary positive mixing quantities.
8. **Writing differences in the wrong order:** Alligation differences are crosswise.
9. **Assuming every alligation ratio represents quantity:** It may represent time, employee count, cost price, marked price, or another weighting quantity.
10. **Using selling price instead of cost price:** If a profit percentage is given, calculate the required cost price before applying alligation where necessary.

# PART A – MIXTURE

## 1. Mixture kya hota hai?

- Hindi: **Mishran**
- Jab **do ya do se zyada alag cheezein** mil jaati hain to mixture banta hai.
- Pani me pani milao to mixture nahi; pani me **doodh/alcohol** milao to mixture.

**Ex:** 50 L milk + 30 L water = **80 L mixture**

---

## 2. Ratio ke Basic Rules (yaad rakho)

### Rule 1: Ratio nikalna
- Milk 50 L, water 32 L → ratio = 50 : 32 = **25 : 16**
- Milk 40 L, water 124 L → 40 : 124 = **10 : 31**

### Rule 2: Quantity add karne par ratio
- Milk 50, water 45 → pani me 20 L add → water = 65 → ratio **50 : 65 = 10 : 13**

### Rule 3: Ek quantity pata ho
- Milk : water = 5 : 7, milk = 20 L → 5 units = 20 → 1 unit = 4 → **water = 7 × 4 = 28 L**

### Rule 4: Mixture ka total (ratio ka sum)
- Milk : water = 7 : 5, water = 15 L
- 5 units = 15 → 1 unit = 3
- **Mixture = (7 + 5) × 3 = 36 L** ("mixture" puchha hai to sum!)

### Rule 5: Total mixture diya ho
- Milk : water = 5 : 3, total = 40 L → 8 units = 40 → 1 unit = 5
- Milk = **25 L**, Water = **15 L**

### Rule 6: Difference diya ho
- Milk : water = 10 : 7, milk − water = 15 L
- 3 units = 15 → 1 unit = 5
- Milk = **50 L**, Water = **35 L**

**Master line:** Jaise hi **1 unit** ki value mil jaye, saari values mil jaati hain.

---

## 3. Pani Add Karne ka Rule ⭐

**Rule:** Agar **pani add** ho raha hai to **milk ka ratio SAME** rehna chahiye. Pani ka ratio badlega.
Milk ke units ko **barabar** karo (cross multiply), phir pani ke units ka difference nikalo.

### Ex1: Milk : water = 2 : 3. 10 L water add karne par 2 : 5 ho gaya.
- Milk same (2 = 2 ✔)
- Water 3 → 5 = **2 units** badhe = 10 L → **1 unit = 5**
- **Old:** Milk 2 × 5 = 10, Water 3 × 5 = 15
- **New:** Milk 10, Water 5 × 5 = 25

### Ex2: Milk : water = 8 : 3. 21 L water add karne par 4 : 5 ho gaya.
- Milk same karo: 4 → 8 (×2): new = 8 : 10
- Water 3 → 10 = **7 units** = 21 → 1 unit = 3
- **Old:** Milk 24, Water 9
- **New:** Milk 24, Water 30

### Ex3: Milk : water = 6 : 5. 66 L water add → 5 : 6 ho gaya.
- Milk same: (6 : 5) × 5 = 30 : 25; (5 : 6) × 6 = 30 : 36
- Water 25 → 36 = **11 units** = 66 → 1 unit = 6
- **Old:** Milk 180, Water 150
- **New:** Milk 180, Water 216

**Note:** Ye "1 unit" sirf **us hisse ke** ratios me use hota hai (old/new alag cases ke beech "dewar/gate" hai, par ek hi unit puri problem me liya ja sakta hai jab same system ho).

---

## 4. Mixture Nikalne ka Rule ⭐⭐

**Rule:** Jab hum mixture me se kuch **nikalte hain**, to **ratio KABHI nahi badalta.**

**Analogy:** Shadi me sharbat ke container se kisi ne bhi gilaas nikala, mithas same rahegi. Kyunki kuch bahar se add nahi hua.

Nikali hui quantity me bhi milk:water **wahi ratio** hota hai.

### Ex: Initial ratio 2 : 3. 50 L mixture nikala. Ratio same (2 : 3).

---

## 5. Nikaal + Pani Add (Combined Case)

### Q: Initial milk : water = 2 : 3. 50 L mixture nikala, phir 12 L water add kiya, ratio ban gaya 2 : 5. Initial mixture, aur final quantities?

**Step 1: Milk same** (kyunki pani add hua) → 2 = 2 ✔
**Step 2:** Water 3 → 5 = 2 units = 12 L → **1 unit = 6**
**Step 3 (Final):** Milk 2 × 6 = 12, Water 5 × 6 = 30, **Total = 42**
**Step 4 (Pani add hone se pehle):** milk 12, water 18 → mixture = **30 L** (ratio 2 : 3 → 5 units = 30)
**Step 5 (Nikaalne se pehle):** 30 + 50 = **80 L** (shuruaati mixture)
**Step 6 (Initial milk & water):** 80 L ka 2 : 3 → 1 unit = 16 → **Milk 32 L, Water 48 L**

**Dhyan:** 6 wali "unit" sirf **add wale hisse** ki hai. Initial wale hisse ko alag unit se nikalo.

### Q (Sanity check): 50 L nikala (2 : 3) → Milk 20, Water 30 ✔ (32 − 12 = 20, 48 − 18 = 30)

---

### Q: 90 L juice : water = 5 : 4. 36 L mixture nikala, same quantity water add kiya. Initial water : final water?
- Initial: 9 units = 90 → 1 unit = 10 → Juice 50, **Water 40**
- 36 L nikala → remaining = 54 L → (5 : 4) → 1 unit = 6 → Juice 30, Water 24
- 36 L pani add → Water = 24 + 36 = **60**
- Initial water : Final water = 40 : 60 = **2 : 3**

### Q: 100 L mixture milk : water = 7 : 3. 10 L nikala, x L water add kiya, naya ratio 21 : 11. 3x = ?
- 10 L nikala → 90 L bacha → Milk 63, Water 27
- Water add: 63 / (27 + x) = 21/11 → 27 + x = 33 → **x = 6**
- **3x = 18 L**

### Q: x L mixture (milk : water = 4 : 3) me se 49 L nikala aur 49 L water add kiya → ratio 2 : 5. Initial mixture?
- Milk same: 2 : 5 → ×2 → 4 : 10
- Water 3 → 10 = 7 units = 49 → 1 unit = 7
- Remaining mixture (nikalne ke baad) = (4 + 3) × 7 = **49 L**
- **Initial = 49 + 49 = 98 L**

### Q: Vessel me milk water = 3 : 2, milk water se 6 L zyada. 50% mixture nikala. Remaining milk?
- 1 unit = 6 L → Milk 18, Water 12
- 50% nikala → har ek ka 50% → **Milk = 9 L**

**Rule:** Mixture ka x% nikalne par har component ka x% nikalta hai.

---

## 6. "A is x% of B" — Base ka Confusion (Bahut Important!)

**Rule:** Jis se compare kiya ja raha hai (jo "of" ke baad hai) wo **base = 100** hota hai.

### Ex1: "Milk is 20% of Water"
- Water (base) = 100, Milk = 20 → ratio **1 : 5**

### Ex2: "Milk is 20% of Mixture"
- Mixture (base) = 100, Milk = 20, Water = 80 → ratio **1 : 4**

### Q: 120 L mixture, milk : water = 7 : 3. Kitna pani add karein ki milk resulting mixture ka 50% ho jaye?
- Milk 84 L, Water 36 L
- 50% milk → milk = water → 84 = 36 + x → **x = 48 L**

## 7. Replacement (Repeated Replacement) ⭐

**Situation:** Vessel me **pure milk** (ya alcohol) hai. Usme se kuch nikala, utna hi pani daala. Ye process kai baar repeat karna hai.

### Basic samajh (ek baar replace)
- 90 L pure milk, 10 L nikala → 80 L milk. 10 L pani add → **Milk : Water = 8 : 1**
- Container fir bhi 90 L ka hi hai (quantity same rahi), bas ab ye **mixture** hai.

### Dusri baar replace
- 90 L pure milk, 18 L nikala → Milk = 72, Water add 18 → **Milk : Water = 72 : 18 = 4 : 1**
- Dobara 18 L nikala (mixture, ratio 4 : 1): 72 L bacha → Milk = 57.6, Water = 14.4
- Water add 18 → Water = 32.4 → **Milk = 57.6, Water = 32.4**

### Shortcut Formula ⭐⭐

**Milk left (n replacements) = Initial × (1 − r/V)ⁿ**

jahan V = total quantity, r = har baar nikali hui quantity, n = kitni baar.

### Ex1: 90 L pure milk, 18 L nikaal ke pani, process 2 baar:
- Fraction = (90 − 18)/90 = 72/90 = **4/5**
- Milk = 90 × (4/5)² = 90 × 16/25 = **57.6 L**
- Water = 90 − 57.6 = **32.4 L**

### Ex2: Wahi, but "process **2 more times**" (total **3 baar**):
- Milk = 90 × (4/5)³ = 90 × 64/125 = **46.08 L**
- Water = 90 − 46.08 = **43.92 L**

**"More times" ka matlab:** "ek baar kiya, phir 2 baar aur" = **total 3 baar** (jab "more" nahi hai to utni hi baar).

---

## 8. Successive Replacement (alag-alag quantity)

### Q: 180 L pure milk. Pehle 18 L nikaal ke pani, phir 15 L mixture nikaal ke pani, phir 10 L mixture nikaal ke pani. Final milk?
- Milk = 180 × (162/180) × (165/180) × (170/180)
- = (162 × 165 × 170)/(180 × 180) = 4,544,100 / 32,400 = **140.25 L**
- (Water = 180 − 140.25 = 39.75 L)

**Dhyan:** Pehli baar sirf milk nikalta hai, baaki baar **mixture** nikalta hai. Isliye har baar fraction = (V − r)/V.

### Q: Vessel me pure alcohol. 6 L nikaal ke pani, 2 baar. Final alcohol : water = 100 : 69. Initial alcohol?
- Final total = 169 units (quantity same hi rahi)
- Alcohol fraction = (1 − 6/V)² = 100/169
- **Square root:** (1 − 6/V) = 10/13 → 6/V = 3/13 → **V = 26 L**
- Ratio trick: Initial 13 units → Final 10 units, 3 units = 6 L → 1 unit = 2 → **13 × 2 = 26 L**

## 9. Alloy Mixing (Copper–Zinc)

### Q1: Alloy A me copper : zinc = 3 : 4, Alloy B me 5 : 9. Barabar quantity mix karo. Naya ratio?

**Galat:** 3 + 5 = 8, 4 + 9 = 13 ❌
**Sahi:** Har alloy ka **fraction** lo
- A me: Copper = 3/7, Zinc = 4/7
- B me: Copper = 5/14, Zinc = 9/14
- Copper = 3/7 + 5/14 = 6/14 + 5/14 = **11/14**
- Zinc = 4/7 + 9/14 = 8/14 + 9/14 = **17/14**
- **Copper : Zinc = 11 : 17**

**Shortcut:** Dono alloys ke total ko **same** karo (7 aur 14 → A ko ×2): A = 6 : 8, B = 5 : 9 → Add → **11 : 17** ✔

### Q2: Wahi alloys, par A aur B ko 2 : 3 ratio me lo. Naya ratio?
- Copper = 2 × (3/7) + 3 × (5/14) = 6/7 + 15/14 = 12/14 + 15/14 = **27/14**
- Zinc = 2 × (4/7) + 3 × (9/14) = 8/7 + 27/14 = 16/14 + 27/14 = **43/14**
- **Copper : Zinc = 27 : 43**

# PART B – ALLIGATION

## 10. Alligation ka Concept

**Idea:** Do values (jaise cost price, speed, profit %) ko mila ke ek **mean (average) value** banti hai. Mean hamesha dono ke **beech** me hoti hai.

### Alligation Rule

Agar **Value 1 < Mean < Value 2**:

| | Value 1 (chhota) | Mean | Value 2 (bada) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diff | **(Value 2 − Mean)** | | **(Mean − Value 1)** |

**Ratio (Quantity 1 : Quantity 2) = (Value 2 − Mean) : (Mean − Value 1)**

**Yaad rakho:** Bade me se mean ghatao (ye chhote ka ratio hai), mean me se chhota ghatao (ye bade ka ratio hai). **Cross subtract.**

**Dhyan:** Mean ke dono taraf ki values ke difference ka ratio batata hai (ulte).

---

## 11. Ratio KIS cheez ka aayega? (Denominator Rule) ⭐

**Rule:** Alligation se jo ratio aata hai, wo us cheez ka hota hai jo **formula ke denominator me hoti hai.**

| Upar kya rakha | Formula | Ratio kiska aayega |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost price / rate (per kg)** | CP = ₹/kg | **Quantity (kg)** ka ratio |
| **Profit % / Loss %** (rupees ka mention ho) | Profit/CP × 100 | **CP** ka ratio |
| **Profit %** (rupees ka mention nahi) | | **Quantity** ka ratio |
| **Average** (marks, salary) | Sum/n | **Number of observations** (persons) ka ratio |
| **Speed** | D/T | **Time** ka ratio |
| **Discount %** | Discount/MRP × 100 | **MRP** ka ratio |
| **Interest %** | Interest/Principal × 100 | **Principal** ka ratio |
| **Bowling avg** (runs per wicket) | Runs/Wicket | **Wickets** ka ratio |
| **Percentage (population)** | x/Total × 100 | **Total (base)** ka ratio |

---

## 12. Solved Alligation Questions

### Q1: Town ki population 2,40,000. Males +7%, females +10%, total +9.5%. Initial males?
- Alligation: (10 − 9.5) : (9.5 − 7) = 0.5 : 2.5 = **1 : 5**
- Ratio **Males : Females = 1 : 5** (percentage → base/total population ka ratio)
- Males = 1/6 × 2,40,000 = **40,000**

### Q2: Do containers me alcohol : water = 3 : 1 aur 3 : 5. Kis ratio me mix karein ki alcohol : water = 5 : 3 ho?
- Alcohol fractions: 3/4, 3/8; Target 5/8
- Denominator 8 karo: 6/8, 3/8, mean = 5/8
- Alligation: (6 − 5) : (5 − 3) = **1 : 2**
- (Water se bhi try karo, answer same: 2/8, 5/8, mean 3/8 → (5 − 3) : (3 − 2) = 2 : 1; pehla container ke liye ratio ulta lagana hai (wahi 1 : 2) ✔)

### Q3: Income −5%, Expenditure −8%, Savings +4%. Income : Expenditure?
- **Rule:** Ek taraf Expenditure, ek taraf Savings, beech me Income (kyunki Income = Exp + Savings)
- Mean = −5, Exp = −8, Sav = +4
- Exp : Sav = (4 − (−5)) : (−5 − (−8)) = 9 : 3 = **3 : 1**
- Income = 3 + 1 = 4 → **Income : Expenditure = 4 : 3**

### Q4: ₹80,000 do banks me. Bank 1: 9.5%, Bank 2: 5.5%. Total interest ₹6400. Bank 1 me kitna?
- Overall % = 6400/80000 × 100 = **8%**
- Ratio (Principal): (8 − 5.5) : (9.5 − 8) = 2.5 : 1.5 = **5 : 3**
- Bank 1 = 5/8 × 80,000 = **₹50,000**

### Q5: Do rice (₹60/kg aur ₹64/kg) kis ratio me mix karein ki ₹68.20/kg par bechne se 10% profit ho?
- **Mean CP chahiye, SP nahi!** SP = 110% of CP → CP = 68.20 × 100/110 = **₹62**
- Alligation: (64 − 62) : (62 − 60) = 2 : 2 = **1 : 1**

### Q6: Boxes (4% profit) aur pens (7% profit), overall profit 6%. Total CP ₹300. Boxes = 20, Pens = 16. Cost per box and pen?
- Alligation: (7 − 6) : (6 − 4) = **1 : 2** (profit% ke saath rupees → **CP ka ratio**)
- Boxes CP = 100, Pens CP = 200
- Box = 100/20 = **₹5**; Pen = 200/16 = **₹12.50**

### Q7: 700 employees. Males avg ₹4200, Females avg ₹4100. Overall avg ₹4190. Females?
- (4190 − 4100) : (4200 − 4190) = 90 : 10 = **9 : 1** (Males : Females) — average → **number of persons**
- Females = 1/10 × 700 = **70**

### Q8: Ek aadmi 20 km/h se 30 min, y km/h se 45 min chala. Avg speed 14 km/h. y = ?
- Speed → **time** ka ratio
- 20 km/h ke liye time 30, y ke liye 45 → Time ratio (y : 20) = 45 : 30 = **3 : 2**
- Alligation: (20 − 14) : (14 − y) = 6 : (14 − y) = 3 : 2 → 14 − y = 4 → **y = 10 km/h**

### Q9: Seller do horses sells. Total MRP ₹4200. Do discounts alag-alag, total discount 12% (gaps 4 aur 3). First horse ka MRP?
- Discount → **MRP** ka ratio
- Ratio = **3 : 4** (total 7 units = 4200 → 1 unit = 600)
- **First horse MRP = 3 × 600 = ₹1800**

### Q10: Bowler ka avg 11.5 runs/wicket. Agle innings me 5 wickets, 45 runs (avg 9). Overall avg 5 kam hokar 11 ho gaya. Pehle ke wickets?
- Bowling avg → **wickets** ka ratio
- Mean = 11; Old avg = 11.5, New avg = 9
- Alligation: Old : New = (11 − 9) : (11.5 − 11) = 2 : 0.5 = **4 : 1**
- New wickets = 5 → **Old wickets = 20**

## 13. Quick Revision Cheat-Sheet

### Mixture
| Situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Pani add | **Milk constant** rakho, pani ka difference unit |
| Nikalna | **Ratio same** rehta hai |
| Nikaal + add | Pehle add wala hissa solve, phir peeche aao |
| "A is x% of B" | B ko 100 maano |
| Replacement (pure se) | Milk = Initial × (1 − r/V)ⁿ |
| Successive replacement | Fractions multiply |
| Alloys | Pehle fractions, ya totals same karo |

### Alligation
| Cheez | Formula |
|---|---|
| Alligation | (High − Mean) : (Mean − Low) |
| Ratio kiska | Denominator ka (CP→quantity; speed→time; avg→persons; discount→MRP; profit%→CP) |

---

## 14. Common Mistakes

1. **Pani add hone par milk ka ratio badal dena** — milk ko barabar karo.
2. **Nikalne par ratio badal dena** — ratio same rehta hai.
3. **1 unit ko galat hisse me use karna** — old aur new alag-alag units hote hain (gate/darwaza concept).
4. **Mixture puchha ho to sirf milk de dena** — total (sum) puchha hai.
5. **"More times" ka matlab bhoolna** — 1 + n more = n + 1 baar.
6. **Replacement formula me V ya r galat** — V = total, r = har baar nikali hui quantity.
7. **Alligation me SP ko mean maan lena** — Profit wale case me **CP** mean hota hai.
8. **Ratio kiska hai ye bhoolna** — denominator wali cheez dekho.
9. **Alloy ko 3 + 5, 4 + 9 add karna** — fractions lena zaroori hai.
10. **Alligation me cross subtract bhoolna** — bade me se mean, mean me se chhota.

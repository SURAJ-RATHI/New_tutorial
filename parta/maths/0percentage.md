# 📘 Percentage (प्रतिशतता) — Complete Notes

## 1. What is Percentage?

Percentage means **per hundred**.

- Percent (%) means out of 100.
- 25% means 25 out of 100.
- 50% means half.
- 100% means the complete value.

### Formula

Percentage = (Part / Total) × 100

### Example

A student scores 40 marks out of 50.

Percentage = (40 / 50) × 100 = 80%

## 3. Convert Percentage, Fraction and Decimal

### Percentage to Decimal

Divide by 100.

Example:

35% = 35/100 = 0.35

### Decimal to Percentage

Multiply by 100.

Example:

0.75 × 100 = 75%

### Fraction to Percentage

Multiply the fraction by 100.

Example:

3/5 × 100 = 60%

### Percentage to Fraction

Write the percentage over 100 and simplify.

Example:

40% = 40/100 = 2/5

## 4. Fast Calculation Tricks

### Trick 1: a% of b = b% of a

This is one of the most useful shortcuts.

Example:

25% of 80

= 80% of 25

= 20

### Trick 2: Break the Percentage

Example: Find 35% of 200.

35% = 30% + 5%

30% of 200 = 60

5% of 200 = 10

Answer = 60 + 10 = 70

### Trick 3: Use the Complement

Example: Find 98% of 500.

98% = 100% - 2%

100% of 500 = 500

2% of 500 = 10

Answer = 500 - 10 = 490

### Trick 4: Find 1% First

Example: Find 17% of 300.

1% of 300 = 3

17% of 300 = 17 × 3 = 51

## 5. Percentage Increase

When a value increases, use:

Increase % = (Increase / Original Value) × 100

New Value = Original Value × (1 + Increase%/100)

### Example

A product costs ₹500. Its price increases by 20%.

Increase = 20% of 500 = ₹100

New price = 500 + 100 = ₹600

### Multiplier Trick

| Change | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| Increase by 10% | 1.10 |
| Increase by 20% | 1.20 |
| Increase by 25% | 1.25 |
| Increase by 50% | 1.50 |
| Increase by 100% | 2.00 |

Example:

₹800 increases by 15%.

New value = 800 × 1.15 = ₹920

---

## 6. Percentage Decrease

Decrease % = (Decrease / Original Value) × 100

New Value = Original Value × (1 - Decrease%/100)

### Example

A product costs ₹800. Its price decreases by 25%.

Decrease = 25% of 800 = ₹200

New price = 800 - 200 = ₹600

### Multiplier Trick

| Change | Multiplier |
|---|---|
| Decrease by 10% | 0.90 |
| Decrease by 20% | 0.80 |
| Decrease by 25% | 0.75 |
| Decrease by 40% | 0.60 |
| Decrease by 50% | 0.50 |

Example:

A value of 600 decreases by 15%.

New value = 600 × 0.85 = 510

## 7. Original Value and New Value

Sometimes the question gives the new value and asks for the original value.

### Formula

After an increase of x%:

Original Value = New Value × 100 / (100 + x)

After a decrease of x%:

Original Value = New Value × 100 / (100 - x)

### Example 1: After Increase

A number becomes 240 after increasing by 20%. Find the original number.

Original = 240 × 100 / 120

Original = 200

### Example 2: After Decrease

A number becomes 360 after decreasing by 10%. Find the original number.

Original = 360 × 100 / 90

Original = 400

**Important:** Calculate the percentage using the correct base. An increase and a decrease of the same percentage do not generally cancel each other.

## 8. Successive Percentage Changes

When a value changes more than once, use multipliers.

### Formula

Net change % = (a + b + ab/100)%

Here:
- Use positive values for increases.
- Use negative values for decreases.

### Example 1: Increase Followed by Increase

A value increases by 20%, then by 10%.

Net change = 20 + 10 + (20 × 10)/100

= 20 + 10 + 2

= 32% increase

### Example 2: Increase Followed by Decrease

A value increases by 20%, then decreases by 10%.

Net change = 20 - 10 + (20 × -10)/100

= 10 - 2

= 8% increase

### Example 3: Increase and Decrease by the Same Percentage

A value increases by 20%, then decreases by 20%.

Net change = 20 - 20 - (20 × 20)/100

= -4%

Answer: 4% decrease.

**Trick:** For multiple changes, multiplying the multipliers is often the easiest method.

Example:

100 → increase by 20% → 120

120 → decrease by 10% → 108

Net increase = 8%

## 9. What Percentage Is A of B?

### Formula

A is what percentage of B?

= (A / B) × 100

### Example

30 is what percentage of 120?

= (30 / 120) × 100

= 25%

**Remember:** The value after "of" is usually the base.

## 10. More Than and Less Than

These questions are tricky because the base changes.

### Example 1

A is 25% more than B.

Assume B = 100.

A = 125.

Question: B is what percentage less than A?

Difference = 125 - 100 = 25

Percentage less = (25 / 125) × 100

= 20%

Therefore, B is 20% less than A.

### Example 2

A is 20% less than B.

Assume B = 100.

A = 80.

Question: B is what percentage more than A?

Difference = 100 - 80 = 20

Percentage more = (20 / 80) × 100

= 25%

Therefore, B is 25% more than A.

### Quick Formula

If A is x% more than B, then B is:

x / (100 + x) × 100 % less than A.

If A is x% less than B, then B is:

x / (100 - x) × 100 % more than A.

## 11. Population Questions

Population questions may involve different percentage increases for different groups.

### Example

A town has 1,000 males and 800 females.

Male population increases by 10%.

Female population increases by 20%.

Find the new total population.

New males = 1,000 × 1.10 = 1,100

New females = 800 × 1.20 = 960

New total = 1,100 + 960 = 2,060

Old total = 1,000 + 800 = 1,800

Increase = 2,060 - 1,800 = 260

Percentage increase = (260 / 1,800) × 100

= 14.44...%

**Trick:** Calculate each group's new population separately, then add them. Do not simply average the percentages unless the group sizes justify it.

## 12. Income, Expenditure and Savings

### Main Relationship

Income = Expenditure + Savings

Savings = Income - Expenditure

Expenditure = Income - Savings

### Example

A person's income is ₹20,000. They spend 70% of their income.

Expenditure = 70% of 20,000 = ₹14,000

Savings = 20,000 - 14,000 = ₹6,000

Savings percentage = (6,000 / 20,000) × 100

= 30%

### When Expenditure Is a Percentage More Than Savings

Suppose expenditure is 40% more than savings.

Assume savings = 100.

Expenditure = 140.

Total income = 100 + 140 = 240.

Savings percentage of income = (100 / 240) × 100

= 41.66...%

**Trick:** If the question compares expenditure with savings, first assume a convenient value for savings. Then calculate expenditure and income.

## 13. Price and Consumption

These questions often ask how consumption must change when price changes and total expenditure remains constant.

### Formula

If price increases by x%, required decrease in consumption is:

Decrease % = x / (100 + x) × 100

If price decreases by x%, possible increase in consumption is:

Increase % = x / (100 - x) × 100

### Example 1

Price increases by 25%. Find the required decrease in consumption to keep expenditure constant.

Decrease = 25 / 125 × 100

= 20%

### Example 2

Price decreases by 20%. Find the possible increase in consumption.

Increase = 20 / 80 × 100

= 25%

**Reason:** Total expenditure = Price × Quantity.

---

## 14. Election and Voting Questions

Read carefully whether the percentage is based on:
- Total voters
- Votes cast
- Valid votes
- Invalid votes

### Example

A candidate gets 60% of 5,000 valid votes. Find the votes received.

Votes received = 60% of 5,000

= 3,000 votes

Other candidate's votes = 5,000 - 3,000

= 2,000 votes

Winning margin = 3,000 - 2,000

= 1,000 votes

### When Invalid Votes Are Given

Suppose 10% of 6,000 votes are invalid.

Invalid votes = 10% of 6,000 = 600

Valid votes = 6,000 - 600 = 5,400

If a candidate receives 60% of valid votes:

Candidate's votes = 60% of 5,400 = 3,240

**Trick:** First calculate valid votes if the question gives invalid votes. Then apply the candidate's percentage to valid votes.

---

## 15. Pass and Fail Questions

### Example

In an examination, 70% of 200 students pass.

Passed students = 70% of 200 = 140

Failed students = 200 - 140 = 60

Percentage failed = 100% - 70% = 30%

**Trick:** If every student is either passed or failed:

Pass percentage + Fail percentage = 100%

---

## 16. Commission Questions

Commission questions may involve different rates for different sales amounts or portions.

### Example

A salesperson receives a 5% commission on sales of ₹10,000.

Commission = 5% of 10,000

= ₹500

### Different Commission Rates

Suppose:
- First ₹10,000 of sales earns 5%.
- The next ₹5,000 earns 10%.

Commission on first ₹10,000 = ₹500

Commission on next ₹5,000 = ₹500

Total commission = ₹1,000

**Trick:** If different rates apply to different portions, calculate each portion separately and add the commissions.

Read the question carefully to determine whether the rate applies to the entire sales amount or only to the amount above a threshold.

## 17. Venn Diagram and Overlapping Groups

Questions may involve people who like one subject, another subject, or both.

### Formula

People in A or B = People in A + People in B - People in both

In symbols:

n(A ∪ B) = n(A) + n(B) - n(A ∩ B)

### Example

30 students like Maths.

25 students like Science.

10 students like both.

Find how many students like at least one subject.

= 30 + 25 - 10

= 45 students

### Only Maths

Only Maths = Total who like Maths - Both

= 30 - 10

= 20 students

### Only Science

Only Science = Total who like Science - Both

= 25 - 10

= 15 students

**Common mistake:** "Maths" may include students who like both subjects. "Only Maths" excludes students who like both.

---

## 18. Find the Original Number from a Percentage

### Example

After a 25% increase, a number becomes 500. Find the original number.

Original = 500 × 100 / 125

= 400

### Example

After a 20% decrease, a number becomes 640. Find the original number.

Original = 640 × 100 / 80

= 800

**Trick:** Do not subtract or add the percentage directly to the final value. Reverse the percentage using the original-value formula.

---

## 19. Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. Always identify the base value before calculating a percentage.
2. A 20% increase followed by a 20% decrease does not return to the original value.
3. "A is 20% more than B" is not the same as "B is 20% less than A."
4. For population questions, calculate each group's change separately.
5. For elections, distinguish total votes from valid votes.
6. In Venn diagrams, subtract the overlap when calculating the total in either group.
7. For original-value questions, use the reverse-percentage formula.
8. For successive changes, use multipliers or the successive-change formula.
9. For price and consumption questions, remember that expenditure = price × quantity.
10. Do not average percentage changes blindly when the original group sizes differ.

# ✅ Answers with Solutions

### 1. Find 25% of 640.

25% = 1/4

640 / 4 = 160

**Answer: 160**

### 2. Convert 12.5% into a fraction.

12.5/100 = 125/1000 = 1/8

**Answer: 1/8**

### 3. Increase from 400 to 500.

Increase = 500 - 400 = 100

Percentage increase = (100 / 400) × 100

**Answer: 25%**

### 4. ₹800 decreases by 15%.

New price = 800 × 0.85

**Answer: ₹680**

### 5. New number is 360 after a 20% increase.

Original = 360 × 100 / 120

**Answer: 300**

### 6. Increase by 10%, then 20%.

Net change = 10 + 20 + (10 × 20)/100

= 32%

**Answer: 32% increase**

### 7. A is 25% more than B.

Assume B = 100.

A = 125.

Percentage by which B is less than A:

25/125 × 100 = 20%

**Answer: 20%**

### 8. Income ₹30,000; expenditure is 80%.

Expenditure = ₹24,000

Savings = ₹30,000 - ₹24,000

**Answer: ₹6,000**

### 9. Price increases by 25%.

Required consumption decrease = 25/125 × 100

**Answer: 20%**

### 10. Total votes = 8,000; invalid votes = 10%.

Valid votes = 90% of 8,000

**Answer: 7,200**

### 11. Maths = 40, Science = 30, Both = 15.

At least one = 40 + 30 - 15

**Answer: 55 students**

### 12. Increase by 20%, then decrease by 20%.

100 → 120 → 96

Net change = 4% decrease

**Answer: 4% decrease**

### 13. Male = 2,000; Female = 1,000.

New males = 2,000 × 1.10 = 2,200

New females = 1,000 × 1.20 = 1,200

New total = 2,200 + 1,200

**Answer: 3,400**

### 14. Commission = 8% of ₹25,000.

= 0.08 × 25,000

**Answer: ₹2,000**

### 15. Number becomes 600 after a 25% decrease.

Original = 600 × 100 / 75

**Answer: 800**

# 🎯 Final Exam Strategy

1. Memorize common percentage-to-fraction conversions.
2. Identify the base value before calculating.
3. Use fractions when the percentage has a familiar fraction equivalent.
4. Use multipliers for successive increases and decreases.
5. Reverse the percentage when the original value is unknown.
6. In word problems, write down the relationship before calculating.
7. Practice different patterns: population, income and savings, elections, commission, price and consumption, and overlapping groups.
8. Check whether the question asks for a percentage, a number, a difference, or an original value.

**Golden Rule:** Percentage questions become easier when you identify the correct base, convert the percentage into a fraction or multiplier, and then calculate.

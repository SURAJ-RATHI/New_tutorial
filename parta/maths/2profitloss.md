# 1. Basic Terminology

## 1.1 Cost Price (CP)

The price at which an article is purchased.

**Example:**

You buy a pen for ₹50.

- Cost Price (CP) = ₹50

Hindi: क्रय मूल्य

## 1.2 Selling Price (SP)

The price at which an article is sold.

**Example:**

You buy a pen for ₹50 and sell it for ₹70.

- CP = ₹50
- SP = ₹70

Hindi: विक्रय मूल्य

## 1.3 Marked Price (MP)

The price printed or displayed on an article before a discount.

**Example:**

A shirt has a price tag of ₹1,000, but the shopkeeper sells it for ₹800.

- MP = ₹1,000
- SP = ₹800

## 1.4 Discount

The reduction given on the marked price.

**Formula:**

Discount = MP - SP

**Example:**

MP = ₹1,000  
SP = ₹800

Discount = 1,000 - 800 = ₹200

# 2. Profit and Loss

## 2.1 Profit

When an article is sold for more than its cost price, a profit occurs.

**Condition:**

SP > CP

**Formula:**

Profit = SP - CP

**Example:**

CP = ₹500  
SP = ₹700

Profit = 700 - 500 = ₹200

Therefore, the profit is ₹200.

## 2.2 Loss

When an article is sold for less than its cost price, a loss occurs.

**Condition:**

SP < CP

**Formula:**

Loss = CP - SP

**Example:**

CP = ₹1,000  
SP = ₹700

Loss = 1,000 - 700 = ₹300

Therefore, the loss is ₹300.

## 2.3 No Profit, No Loss

When the selling price equals the cost price:

SP = CP

Profit = ₹0  
Loss = ₹0

### Remember

- SP > CP → Profit
- SP < CP → Loss
- SP = CP → No profit, no loss

# 3. Profit Percentage and Loss Percentage

## 3.1 Profit Percentage

Profit percentage is always calculated on the cost price in standard profit-and-loss questions.

**Formula:**

Profit % = (Profit / CP) × 100

**Example:**

CP = ₹800  
SP = ₹1,000

Profit = 1,000 - 800 = ₹200

Profit % = (200 / 800) × 100

Profit % = 25%

## 3.2 Loss Percentage

**Formula:**

Loss % = (Loss / CP) × 100

**Example:**

CP = ₹1,000  
SP = ₹700

Loss = 1,000 - 700 = ₹300

Loss % = (300 / 1,000) × 100

Loss % = 30%

### Important Rule

Both standard profit percentage and standard loss percentage use CP in the denominator.

Do not divide by SP unless the question specifically says that profit or loss is calculated on the selling price.

---

# 4. Finding Selling Price (SP)

## 4.1 When Profit Percentage Is Given

**Formula:**

SP = CP × (100 + Profit %) / 100

**Example:**

CP = ₹1,500  
Profit = 10%

SP = 1,500 × 110 / 100

SP = ₹1,650

### Shortcut

For a 10% profit:

SP = CP × 110/100

For a 20% profit:

SP = CP × 120/100

For a 25% profit:

SP = CP × 125/100

## 4.2 When Loss Percentage Is Given

**Formula:**

SP = CP × (100 - Loss %) / 100

**Example:**

CP = ₹1,600  
Loss = 20%

SP = 1,600 × 80 / 100

SP = ₹1,280

### Shortcut

For a 20% loss:

SP = CP × 80/100

For a 30% loss:

SP = CP × 70/100

For a 15% loss:

SP = CP × 85/100

# 5. Finding Cost Price (CP)

## 5.1 When SP and Profit Percentage Are Given

**Formula:**

CP = SP × 100 / (100 + Profit %)

**Example:**

SP = ₹1,200  
Profit = 20%

CP = 1,200 × 100 / 120

CP = ₹1,000

## 5.2 When SP and Loss Percentage Are Given

**Formula:**

CP = SP × 100 / (100 - Loss %)

**Example:**

SP = ₹800  
Loss = 20%

CP = 800 × 100 / 80

CP = ₹1,000

### Remember

- Finding SP with profit → Add the percentage to 100.
- Finding SP with loss → Subtract the percentage from 100.
- Finding CP with profit → Divide by (100 + profit percentage).
- Finding CP with loss → Divide by (100 - loss percentage).

# 6. Different Approaches to Profit and Loss

The lesson explains several approaches. You can use whichever is easiest for you.

## Approach 1: Basic or NCERT Approach

Calculate the profit or loss first, then calculate the percentage.

**Example:**

CP = ₹800  
Profit = 10%

Profit = 10% of 800 = ₹80

SP = 800 + 80 = ₹880

This approach is useful when you want to understand the concept clearly.

## Approach 2: Zoom or Fraction Approach

Convert the percentage into a fraction.

Examples:

- 10% = 1/10
- 20% = 1/5
- 25% = 1/4
- 30% = 3/10
- 40% = 2/5
- 12.5% = 1/8
- 16⅔% = 1/6

### Example: 10% Profit

CP = ₹1,500

A 10% profit means the profit is 1/10 of CP.

Profit = 1,500/10 = ₹150

SP = 1,500 + 150 = ₹1,650

## Approach 3: Chain or Multiplier Approach

This is a fast approach for competitive exams.

### Profit

SP = CP × (100 + Profit %) / 100

### Loss

SP = CP × (100 - Loss %) / 100

**Example: 10% Profit**

CP = ₹1,500

SP = 1,500 × 110/100

SP = ₹1,650

**Example: 20% Loss**

CP = ₹1,600

SP = 1,600 × 80/100

SP = ₹1,280

## Approach 4: Ratio Approach

Assume CP = 100 units.

For 10% profit:

CP : SP = 100 : 110 = 10 : 11

For 20% loss:

CP : SP = 100 : 80 = 5 : 4

### Example

CP = ₹1,500 and profit = 10%.

CP : SP = 10 : 11

10 units = ₹1,500

1 unit = ₹150

SP = 11 × 150 = ₹1,650

### When to Use Ratio Approach

Use it when:
- The question involves multiple articles.
- Different profit or loss percentages are given.
- You need to compare two selling prices.
- Actual values are not directly provided.

# 7. Successive Profit and Loss

Successive profit or loss means that the same article passes through more than one transaction.

For example:
- A person buys an article.
- He sells it at a profit.
- The next person sells it again at another profit.

Each percentage applies to the value at that stage.

## 7.1 Successive Profit

**Example:**

An article costs ₹1,500. It is sold at a 10% profit. The buyer then sells it at a 20% profit.

First transaction:

SP₁ = 1,500 × 110/100

SP₁ = ₹1,650

Second transaction:

SP₂ = 1,650 × 120/100

SP₂ = ₹1,980

Final selling price = ₹1,980

### Direct Formula

Final value = Initial value × (1 + p/100) × (1 + q/100)

Here, p and q are profit percentages.

For two successive profits:

Equivalent profit % = p + q + (pq/100)

For 10% and 20%:

Equivalent profit = 10 + 20 + (10 × 20)/100

Equivalent profit = 32%

So the final value is:

1,500 × 132/100 = ₹1,980

**Important:** Two successive profits of 10% and 20% give a total profit of 32%, not 30%.

## 7.2 Successive Loss

For two successive losses:

Equivalent loss % = p + q - (pq/100)

**Example:**

An article suffers successive losses of 10% and 20%.

Equivalent loss = 10 + 20 - (10 × 20)/100

Equivalent loss = 28%

## 7.3 Profit Followed by Loss

Use multipliers.

For 10% profit followed by 20% loss:

Final value = Initial value × 110/100 × 80/100

= Initial value × 0.88

Therefore, the overall result is a 12% loss.

### General Rule

- Profit → Multiply by (100 + p)/100
- Loss → Multiply by (100 - p)/100

For three or more transactions, multiply all the appropriate factors.

# 8. Questions with Two Selling Prices

These questions give different selling prices for different profit or loss percentages.

## Example 1: Find the Selling Price for a Desired Profit

An article is sold for ₹284 at a loss of 29%. At what price should it be sold to gain 35%?

### Step 1: Convert percentages into ratios

At 29% loss:

CP : SP = 100 : 71

At 35% profit:

CP : SP = 100 : 135

### Step 2: Use the known selling price

71 ratio units = ₹284

1 unit = 284/71 = ₹4

### Step 3: Find the required selling price

Required SP = 135 × 4

Required SP = ₹540

**Answer: ₹540**

## Example 2: Difference Between Two Selling Prices

An article is sold at a 15% profit in one case and a 17% loss in another. The difference between the two selling prices is ₹64. Find the selling price at a 10% profit.

Assume CP = 100 units.

At 15% profit:

SP₁ = 115 units

At 17% loss:

SP₂ = 83 units

Difference = 115 - 83 = 32 units

32 units = ₹64

1 unit = ₹2

CP = 100 × 2 = ₹200

At 10% profit:

SP = 110 × 2

SP = ₹220

**Answer: ₹220**

### Shortcut

When the two cases are one profit and one loss:

Difference in ratio units = Profit % + Loss %

In the example:

15 + 17 = 32 units

Be careful: this shortcut works when both percentages are calculated on the same CP.

# 9. Questions Based on Number of Articles

These questions use the cost price or selling price of multiple articles.

## Example 1: CP of 15 Articles Equals SP of 10 Articles

The cost price of 15 articles equals the selling price of 10 articles. Find the loss percentage.

Let CP of one article = C and SP of one article = S.

15C = 10S

Therefore:

S/C = 15/10 = 3/2

This means SP is greater than CP.

So this condition actually gives a profit, not a loss.

Profit ratio = 3 - 2 = 1

Profit % = (1/2) × 100 = 50%

**Answer: 50% profit**

### Important Lesson

Always write the equation exactly as given.

If:

- CP of 15 articles = SP of 10 articles,

then:

15C = 10S

Do not reverse the numbers. Reversing them changes the answer.

## Example 2: CP of 10 Articles Equals SP of 15 Articles

10C = 15S

S/C = 10/15 = 2/3

CP : SP = 3 : 2

Loss ratio = 3 - 2 = 1

Loss % = (1/3) × 100

Loss = 33⅓%

**Answer: 33⅓% loss**

## Example 3: Selling 2 Articles Gives a Profit Equal to CP of 5 Articles

A person earns a profit equal to the cost price of 5 articles by selling 2 articles. Find the profit percentage.

Let CP of one article = C and SP of one article = S.

Profit on 2 articles:

2S - 2C = 5C

2S = 7C

Therefore:

S/C = 7/2

Profit on one article = 7 - 2 = 5 ratio units.

Profit % = (5/2) × 100

Profit = 250%

**Answer: 250% profit**

### Important Concept

If a question explicitly states that profit or loss occurred after selling a certain number of articles, the number purchased is often implied to be the same number, unless the question says otherwise.

Read the statement carefully before making this assumption.

# 10. Profit Calculated on Selling Price

This is a special type of question.

Normally:

Profit % = (Profit / CP) × 100

But a question may say that profit is 20% of SP.

Then:

Profit / SP = 20/100

Let SP = 5 units.

Profit = 1 unit.

Since:

SP = CP + Profit

CP = 5 - 1 = 4 units

Actual profit percentage:

Profit % = (1/4) × 100

Profit = 25%

**Answer: 25% actual profit**

### Shortcut Formula

If profit is p% of SP:

Actual profit % = [p / (100 - p)] × 100

For 20% profit on SP:

= 20/80 × 100

= 25%

### If Loss Is Calculated on SP

If loss is p% of SP:

Actual loss % = [p / (100 + p)] × 100

Example: Loss is 20% of SP.

Actual loss % = 20/120 × 100

= 16⅔%

### Remember

Check the wording carefully:

- Profit on CP → Standard profit percentage.
- Profit on SP → Use SP as the base.
- Loss on CP → Standard loss percentage.
- Loss on SP → Use the special formula.

# 11. Equal Profit and Loss

A common question says:

The profit from selling an article at one price equals the loss from selling the same article at another price.

## Example

An article gives:
- Profit when sold for ₹134.
- An equal amount of loss when sold for ₹116.

Find the CP.

Let CP = C and equal profit/loss = x.

When sold at ₹134:

C + x = 134

When sold at ₹116:

C - x = 116

Add both equations:

2C = 134 + 116

2C = 250

C = ₹125

**Answer: ₹125**

### Shortcut Formula

When profit and loss amounts are equal:

CP = (Higher SP + Lower SP) / 2

CP = (134 + 116)/2 = ₹125

### Find the Equal Profit or Loss

x = (Higher SP - Lower SP)/2

x = (134 - 116)/2

x = ₹9

Therefore:
- CP = ₹125
- Profit at ₹134 = ₹9
- Loss at ₹116 = ₹9

# 12. Overall Profit and Loss

When several articles or transactions are involved, calculate the total cost and total selling price.

**Formula:**

Overall Profit = Total SP - Total CP

Overall Loss = Total CP - Total SP

## Example

A shopkeeper buys two articles for ₹500 each.

He sells:
- First article for ₹600
- Second article for ₹400

Total CP = 500 + 500 = ₹1,000

Total SP = 600 + 400 = ₹1,000

Overall result = No profit, no loss.

### Important Lesson

Do not decide the overall result from only one transaction. Consider all articles and their values.

If some articles remain unsold or are wasted, account for their cost when calculating the overall result.

# 13. Marked Price (MP) and Discount

## 13.1 Discount Formula

Discount = MP - SP

Discount % = (Discount / MP) × 100

**Example:**

MP = ₹1,000  
SP = ₹800

Discount = 1,000 - 800 = ₹200

Discount % = (200/1,000) × 100

Discount = 20%

## 13.2 Find SP After Discount

SP = MP × (100 - Discount %)/100

**Example:**

MP = ₹2,000  
Discount = 15%

SP = 2,000 × 85/100

SP = ₹1,700

## 13.3 Find MP When SP and Discount Are Given

MP = SP × 100/(100 - Discount %)

**Example:**

SP = ₹800  
Discount = 20%

MP = 800 × 100/80

MP = ₹1,000

### Quick Ratio Approach

For a 20% discount:

MP : SP = 100 : 80 = 5 : 4

For a 25% discount:

MP : SP = 100 : 75 = 4 : 3

For a 40% discount:

MP : SP = 100 : 60 = 5 : 3

For a 10% discount:

MP : SP = 100 : 90 = 10 : 9

# 14. Successive Discounts

Successive discounts mean that two or more discounts are applied one after another.

The second discount is calculated on the price remaining after the first discount, not on the original marked price.

## Example 1: Discounts of 20%, 40%, and 25%

Assume MP = ₹1,000.

First discount = 20%

Price after first discount:

1,000 × 80/100 = ₹800

Second discount = 40%

Price after second discount:

800 × 60/100 = ₹480

Third discount = 25%

Final selling price:

480 × 75/100 = ₹360

Total discount = 1,000 - 360 = ₹640

Equivalent discount percentage:

(640/1,000) × 100 = 64%

**Answer: 64% equivalent discount**

### Important

20% + 40% + 25% = 85% is incorrect.

The discounts are applied successively, so the correct equivalent discount is 64%.

## Example 2: Two Successive Discounts

For discounts of a% and b%:

Equivalent discount % = a + b - (ab/100)

**Example:**

20% and 10% discounts.

Equivalent discount = 20 + 10 - (20 × 10)/100

= 30 - 2

= 28%

## Example 3: Find the Second Discount

Two successive discounts give an overall discount of 25%. The first discount is 10%. Find the second discount.

Assume MP = ₹100.

After the first discount of 10%:

Price = ₹90

Overall discount is 25%, so the final selling price is:

SP = ₹75

The second discount reduces ₹90 to ₹75.

Second discount = 90 - 75 = ₹15

Second discount % = (15/90) × 100

= 16⅔%

**Answer: 16⅔%**

### Successive Discount Multiplier Method

For a discount of d%:

Remaining price multiplier = (100 - d)/100

For two discounts:

Final SP = MP × (100 - a)/100 × (100 - b)/100

This method works for any number of successive discounts.

# 15. Dishonest Shopkeeper and False Weight

A dishonest shopkeeper may cheat by:
- Using less weight than stated.
- Charging a customer for 1 kg but giving less than 1 kg.
- Buying goods using an incorrect weight.
- Increasing the marked price and then offering a discount.

Always separate these situations and calculate the actual amount of goods and money.

## 15.1 Selling Less Than 1 kg as 1 kg

Suppose a shopkeeper charges the price of 1 kg but gives only 800 g.

Assume the cost price of 1 kg is ₹100.

The shopkeeper charges ₹100 but gives goods that cost:

₹100 × 800/1,000 = ₹80

Profit = 100 - 80 = ₹20

Profit % = (20/80) × 100

= 25%

**Answer: 25% profit**

### General Formula

If the shopkeeper gives x grams instead of 1,000 grams and charges the price of 1 kg:

Profit % = [(1,000 - x)/x] × 100

This assumes the shopkeeper's cost per gram is the same and the customer pays the full 1 kg price.

## 15.2 Giving 900 g Instead of 1 kg

Suppose a shopkeeper charges the price of 1 kg but gives 900 g.

Profit % = [(1,000 - 900)/900] × 100

= (100/900) × 100

= 11⅑%

**Answer: 11⅑% profit**

### Remember

The denominator is the actual quantity given, because that quantity determines the shopkeeper's cost.

## 15.3 When the Shopkeeper Also Gets Less While Buying

If a shopkeeper receives less than the stated weight while purchasing and also gives less while selling, calculate the actual quantity received and the actual quantity sold separately.

Do not automatically add the two percentages. First determine:
1. How much money was paid.
2. How much quantity was actually received.
3. How much quantity was actually given to customers.
4. How much money was collected.

Then calculate profit or loss using actual cost and actual revenue.

## 15.4 Marked Price, Discount, and False Weight Together

These questions may combine:
- An increase in marked price.
- A discount on the marked price.
- A false weight while purchasing.
- A false weight while selling.

### Method

1. Assume a convenient original price, such as ₹100.
2. Apply any increase in price.
3. Apply the discount to the new marked price.
4. Calculate the actual quantity purchased or received.
5. Calculate the actual quantity given to customers.
6. Compare actual revenue with actual cost.

**Important:** The exact sequence matters. Apply each percentage to the correct base.

# 16. Common Mistakes

## Mistake 1: Using SP as the denominator

Incorrect for standard profit percentage:

Profit / SP × 100

Correct:

Profit / CP × 100

## Mistake 2: Adding Successive Percentages Directly

Two successive profits of 10% and 20% do not give 30% overall profit.

Correct overall profit = 32%.

## Mistake 3: Adding Successive Discounts Directly

Discounts of 20% and 10% do not give 30% overall discount.

Correct equivalent discount = 28%.

## Mistake 4: Confusing MP and CP

- CP = purchase price.
- MP = displayed or marked price.
- SP = actual selling price.

Discount is calculated on MP, while standard profit or loss percentage is calculated on CP.

## Mistake 5: Ignoring the Number of Articles

If a question involves 10 articles, do not automatically use the price of one article without adjusting the quantity.

## Mistake 6: Reversing a Ratio

If CP of 15 articles equals SP of 10 articles:

15C = 10S

Write the equation first, then simplify.

## Mistake 7: Forgetting the Correct Base

Always identify what the percentage applies to:
- CP?
- SP?
- MP?
- Actual quantity or weight?

The wording of the question determines the base.

# 19. Quick Revision Sheet

## Basic Formulas

Profit = SP - CP

Loss = CP - SP

Profit % = (Profit / CP) × 100

Loss % = (Loss / CP) × 100

Discount = MP - SP

Discount % = (Discount / MP) × 100

## Selling Price

SP at profit p% = CP × (100 + p)/100

SP at loss l% = CP × (100 - l)/100

## Cost Price

CP at profit p% = SP × 100/(100 + p)

CP at loss l% = SP × 100/(100 - l)

## Successive Changes

Two successive profits:

Equivalent profit % = p + q + pq/100

Two successive losses:

Equivalent loss % = p + q - pq/100

For mixed profit and loss, multiply the individual change factors.

## Successive Discounts

Equivalent discount for two discounts:

d = a + b - ab/100

For multiple discounts:

Final SP = MP × (100 - d₁)/100 × (100 - d₂)/100 × ...

## Equal Profit and Loss

CP = (Higher SP + Lower SP)/2

Equal profit or loss amount = (Higher SP - Lower SP)/2

## False Weight

If a shopkeeper charges for 1,000 g but gives x grams:

Profit % = [(1,000 - x)/x] × 100

This assumes the shopkeeper pays the same cost per gram and charges the full 1 kg price.

# Final Exam Strategy

1. Read the question carefully and identify CP, SP, MP, and the percentage.
2. Determine which value is the base for the percentage.
3. Use the direct formula for simple questions.
4. Use the ratio approach when multiple articles or multiple selling prices are involved.
5. Use multipliers for successive profit, loss, and discount questions.
6. For dishonest-shopkeeper questions, calculate actual quantity and actual money first.
7. Check whether the question asks for profit/loss amount or percentage.
8. Practice different approaches, but use the method that is fastest and clearest for you.

**Golden Rule:** Profit and loss percentages are normally calculated on CP; discount percentage is calculated on MP. Always check the wording before applying a formula.

| **SP** | Selling Price (bikri price) |
| **MP** | Marked Price (dukaan par likha price) |
| **Profit** | SP − CP |
| **Loss** | CP − SP |
| **Markup** | CP ke upar price badhana (MP = CP + markup) |
| **Discount** | MP me se price ghatana (SP = MP − discount) |

**Formulas:**

- Profit % = (Profit / CP) × 100
- Loss % = (Loss / CP) × 100
- **Profit/Loss hamesha CP par nikalta hai** (SP ya MP par nahi)
- Discount % = (Discount / MP) × 100

**Golden rule (units wala trick):**
- Profit hai → 100 mein **add** karo: 20% profit → CP : SP = 100 : 120 = **5 : 6**
- Loss hai → 100 mein se **minus** karo: 28% loss → CP : SP = 100 : 72 = **25 : 18**

100 units = CP (hamesha). Isse zyada kuch yaad nahi rakhna.

## 3. Basic Profit/Loss Questions

### Q1. Loss ₹125, loss % = 5% → CP?
- 5% of CP = 125
- 100% (CP) = 125 × 100 / 5 = **₹2500**

> Trick: "Jo bhi % diya hai uski value diya hai, to 100% (CP) nikalo."

### Q2. CP : SP = 432 : 612 → Profit %?
- 9 se divide (digit sum 9 hai): 432 → 48, 612 → 68
- 4 se divide: 12 : 17
- Profit = 5 on 12 → 5/12 × 100 = **41.66% (41⅔%)**

> Digit-sum trick: Agar number ka digit sum 9 hai to wo 9 se divisible hai.

### Q3. 36% gain expected on CP. Profit nikalna hai SP diya hai.
- CP : SP = 100 : 136
- Profit = 36 units, SP = 136 units
- Agar SP diya ho to profit = SP × 36/136
- Unit digit se option eliminate kar sakte ho, poora multiply nahi karna.

## 4. Overhead Charges / Repair Charges

**Concept:** Transport, storage, repair, labour sab **CP me add** hote hain.

### Q4. Items kharide ₹50,000, transport ₹5,000, storage ₹3,000. Bechte hain ₹63,000. Profit %?
- Total CP = 50,000 + 5,000 + 3,000 = **58,000**
- Profit = 63,000 − 58,000 = 5,000
- Profit % = 5000/58000 × 100 ≈ **8.6%**
- Option trick: loss wale options pehle hata do. Phir dekho 7.x hai ya 8.x. 5×100/58 → 8.something.

### Q5. ₹7200 me article kharida, CP ka 9% repair kiya, ₹8100 me beca. Profit %?
- Repair = 9% of 7200 = ₹648
- Total CP = 7200 + 648 = **7848**
- Profit = 8100 − 7848 = 252
- Profit % = 252/7848 × 100 = **3.2%**

## 5. New Selling Price (jab original SP diya ho)

### Q6. Ek aadmi ₹144 me beca, loss 28%. Kitne me bechne se ... (pehle CP nikalo)
- Loss 28% → SP = 72 units = ₹144
- CP (100 units) = 144 × 100/72 = **₹200**
- Agar naya SP ₹288 hai → profit = 88 on 200 → **44% profit**

### Q7. 20% profit par SP diya, CP nikalo, fir naya SP par profit/loss
- 120 units = SP, 100 units = CP
- Jaise yahan CP = ₹4600 aaya
- Naya SP = ₹4370 → Loss = 4600 − 4370 = ₹230
- 230 is 5% of 4600 (10% = 460, aadha = 230) → **5% loss**

> **Do tareeke:** (1) CP nikaal kar new SP se compare karo (safe, option-elimination possible), (2) direct ratio. Pehla tareeka better hai kyunki approximation lag jaata hai.

## 6. Number Line Concept (Bahut Important)

**Idea:** Jab SP badhane/ghatane se profit/loss change hota hai, to % ke difference ko number line par dekho.

### Q8. 15% loss tha. SP ₹3375 badhaya to 12% profit hua. CP?
- Number line: −15% se +12% tak = 15 + 12 = **27%**
- 27% = ₹3375 → CP = 3375 × 100/27 = **₹12,500**

### Q9. Ankita ko watch par 5% loss tha. ₹300 aur milte to 5% profit hota. Watch ka SP?
- −5% se +5% = **10%** = ₹300
- 100% = CP = ₹3000
- Original SP = 3000 − 5% of 3000 = 3000 − 150 = **₹2850**

**Trap:** Yahan "selling price of the watch" pucha gaya hai, CP nahi. Aksar students CP (3000) likh dete hain. Question end tak padho!

### Q10. Rashid ka pehle 20% profit, SP badhane par 37% profit. Difference ₹34.51. CP?
- 37 − 20 = **17%** = ₹34.51
- 100% = 34.51 × 100/17 = **₹203**

**Cases:** loss→profit (dono jodo), profit→profit (subtract karo), loss→loss (subtract karo).

## 7. Equal Profit% = Loss% → CP = Average

**Concept:** Agar ek SP par profit% aur doosre SP par loss% barabar hai, to **CP dono SP ka average** hota hai.

### Q11. Ek SP par profit% = doosre SP (₹1460) par loss%. Pehla SP ₹2100. 20% profit chahiye to SP?
- CP = (2100 + 1460)/2 = **₹1780**
- 20% profit: 1780 + 20% of 1780 = 1780 + 356 = **₹2136**

### Q12. ₹200 (×10 scale) aur ₹100 type: SP dono ka average hi CP hota hai
- Example: CP = 2000. Agar ₹2050 par bechte hain → profit 50 on 2000 = **2.5%**
- Trick: 10% of 2000 = 200, uska ¼ = 50 → 2.5%.

**Note:** Yahan "profit = loss" se matlab hai **amount** ya % dono barabar.

## 8. Article Concept (Quantity Wale)

**Concept:** Same quantity ke liye CP aur SP nikalo, phir compare.

### Q13. 20 dozen kele ₹1200 me kharide, 5 dozen ₹350 me beche. Profit %?
- 1 dozen CP = 1200/20 = ₹60
- 1 dozen SP = 350/5 = ₹70
- Profit = 10 on 60 = 1/6 → **16.66%**

## 9. Defective / Spoiled Items (Fruit Rot, Bulb Fuse etc.)

**Concept:** **Total CP** aur **total SP** nikalo. Defective items ka SP **zero** hota hai (unko phenk diya).

### Q14. 360 bulbs @ ₹18 kharide. 20 fuse nikle. Baaki ₹20 each beche. Profit %?
- Total CP = 360 × 18 = ₹6480
- Bechne wale bulbs = 360 − 20 = 340 → SP = 340 × 20 = ₹6800
- Profit = 6800 − 6480 = 320
- Profit % = 320/6480 = 4/81 → **≈ 4.9%** (5% se thoda kam)
- Option trick: do loss wale options hata do, 5% ke close dekho.

> Yehi language fruits, vegetables, eggs ke saath bhi aati hai.

## 10. Total Profit Wale Questions (Ek item ka profit nikalo)

### Q15. 20 laptop bags ₹13,000 me kharide, 15 bags ₹10,125 me beche. Total profit ₹3225 chahiye to kitne bags?
- 1 bag CP = 13000/20 = ₹650
- 1 bag SP = 10125/15 = ₹675
- 1 bag par profit = ₹25
- Total profit 3225 / 25 = **129 bags**

**Logic:** Ek unit ka profit nikalo, total profit me divide karo.

## 11. "Profit = CP of x kg" Type

### Q16. 15 kg apples ₹750 me beche. Profit = 5 kg apples ke CP ke barabar. Profit %?
- **Rule:** Dekho profit/loss kiske form me diya hai: CP ke form me ya SP ke form me.
- Yahan CP ke form me diya hai → total quantity (15 kg) bhi **CP ke neeche** rakho.
- Profit % = 5/15 × 100 = **33.33%**

(Basic method: 1 kg CP = x, profit = 5x, total CP = 15x → profit/CP = 5x/15x = 1/3 ✓)

## 12. Number of Articles Nikalna

### Q17. 15 toys ₹3000 me beche, 25% profit. Kitne toys ₹2816 me beche jaye ki 10% profit ho?
- 25% profit → CP : SP = 4 : 5 → 5 units = 3000 → 4 units (CP) = ₹2400
- 1 toy CP = 2400/15 = **₹160**
- Naya SP ₹2816, 10% profit → 11 units = 2816 → 10 units (total CP) = **₹2560**
- Toys = 2560/160 = **16 toys**

*(Transcript me SP ₹816 likha tha; check karne par ₹2816 se hi 16 aata hai, isliye ye number use kiya.)*

**Steps:** (1) ek item ka CP nikalo, (2) naye SP se total CP nikalo, (3) total CP ÷ ek item CP = number.

## 13. SP Double Karne Par Profit Multiply

### Q18. SP double karne par profit 4 guna ho jata hai. Original profit %?
- SP double → increase = 1 unit (original SP)
- Profit 4 guna → profit **3 units** badha
- Matlab original SP = 3 units, original profit = 1 unit
- CP = SP − profit = 3 − 1 = **2 units**
- Profit % = 1/2 × 100 = **50%**

**Proof:** CP = 2, SP = 3, profit = 1. Naya SP = 6, naya profit = 4 ✓

## 14. Successive Profit/Loss

**Formula (do baar % change):**

**Net % = a + b + (ab/100)**

(Profit ke liye **+**, loss ke liye **−** sign lagao.)

### Q19. Article do hands se guzarta hai, total profit 40%. Pehle dealer ka profit 30%. Doosre ka?
- 30 + x + 30x/100 = 40
- 13x/10 = 10 → x = 100/13 = **7.69%** (100/13%)
- Multiplying factor se bhi: 1.4/1.3 = 1.0769

### Q20. ₹42,000 me article kharida, 20% loss par beca, us paise se naya article kharida aur 30% profit par beca. Overall?
- Net = −20 + 30 + (−20×30)/100 = 10 − 6 = **+4%**
- 4% of 42,000 = **₹1680 profit**

> Plus = profit, minus = loss.

---

## 15. Weighted Mean / Allegation Type (HFT)

**Concept:** Quantity (ya CP) jo **weight** represent karta hai, uska ratio lo. Fir weighted average lagao.

**Formula:** Overall % = (n₁a₁ + n₂a₂)/(n₁ + n₂)

### Q21. 100 pens @ ₹10. 60 pens 20% profit par, baaki 40 pens 10% loss par. Overall?
- Quantity ratio 60 : 40 = **3 : 2**
- (3 × 20 + 2 × (−10)) / 5 = 40/5 = **8% profit**

> Quantity ka ratio hamesha lo, calculation simple ho jati hai.

### Q22. Laptop ₹56,000 aur printer ₹22,000. Laptop 20% profit, printer 15% profit par. Overall profit %?
- CP ratio = 56 : 22 = **28 : 11**
- Overall = (28×20 + 11×15)/39 = (560 + 165)/39 = 725/39 ≈ **18.6%**
- Shortcut: Answer 15% aur 20% ke **beech** hoga. Denominator 39 hoga. Option se match karo.

### Q23. ₹1500 ke watermelons. 1/4 quantity 12% loss par beche. Baaki kis profit % par beche ki overall 25% profit ho?
- Quantity ratio = 1 : 3
- (1 × (−12) + 3 × x)/4 = 25
- −12 + 3x = 100 → 3x = 112 → **x = 37.33%** (112/3 %)

---

## 16. Dishonest Shopkeeper (Multiplying Factor)

**Concept:** Jo **actual weight** use karta hai wo **denominator** me likho.

**Format:** MF = (Price factor) × (Claimed weight / Actual weight used)

### Q24. Cost price par bechne ka dawa, par 1 kg ke badle 920 g use karta hai. Profit %?
- MF = 1000/920 = 25/23
- Profit = 2/23 × 100 = **8.69%** (≈ 8.7%)

### Q25. 5% loss par apple beche, lekin 20% kam weight use karta hai. Overall profit?
- Price MF = 95/100
- Weight (kam use) = 100 ghar ke badle 80 g → 100/80
- Net MF = (95/100)(100/80) = 95/80 = **19/16**
- Profit = 3/16 = **18.75%**

> Yaad rakho: 1/16 = 6.25%, to 3/16 = 18.75%, 5/16 = 31.25%, 7/16 = 43.75%, 9/16 = 56.25%. Ye fractions exam me bahut kaam aate hain.

### Q26. 25% loss par beche, 45% kam weight use karta hai. Profit %?
- Price MF = 3/4
- Weight: 100 ke badle 55 → 100/55
- Net MF = (3/4)(100/55) = 15/11
- Profit = 4/11 = **36.36%**

### Q27. Oil par 40% markup, 15% kam measure karne wali machine, 32% discount. Overall?
- Markup MF = 7/5
- Discount MF = 68/100 = 17/25
- Weight MF = 100/85 = 20/17
- Net = (7/5)(17/25)(20/17) = 28/25
- Profit = 3/25 = **12%**

**Weight ke liye rule:** Agar x% kam weight use kare to 100 ke badle (100 − x) use hota hai → factor 100/(100−x).

## 17. Discount Chapter

### 17.1 Markup + Discount (Successive)

**Formula (profit/markup +, discount −):**

**Net = a + b + (ab/100)** (discount ko negative sign ke saath rakho)

### Q28. 25% markup, 10% discount. Profit?
- 25 − 10 + (25 × (−10))/100 = 15 − 2.5 = **12.5% profit**

### Q29. Bag par 25% markup, 18% discount. Profit?
- 25 − 18 + (25 × (−18))/100 = 7 − 4.5 = **2.5% profit**

**Yaad rakho:** Markup = price badhana, Discount = price ghatana. Back-to-back hota hai isliye successive formula lagta hai.

### 17.2 Successive Discount (Sabse Zyada Poocha Jaata Hai)

**Formula (single equivalent discount):**

**Net Discount = a + b − (ab/100)**

Ya **Multiplying Factor** method (zyada fast): Final = MP × (1 − d₁)(1 − d₂)...

### Q30. 60%, 70%, 80% discounts ka single discount?
- MF = (4/10)(3/10)(2/10) = 24/1000 = 0.024
- Discount = 1 − 0.024 = 0.976 = **97.6%**

### Q31. Teen successive 25% discounts. Single discount?
- MF = (3/4)³ = 27/64
- Discount = 37/64 × 100 = **57.8%**
- Formula se do baar lagana padega, isliye yahan MF fast hai.

> **Lesson:** Kab kaunsa method? Chhote numbers/fractions ho to MF. Direct formula do discount ke liye.

### Q32. 25% discount aur do successive discounts (20% & 15%) me difference ₹28. Bill amount?
- 20 + 15 − (20×15)/100 = 35 − 3 = **32%**
- Difference = 32% − 25% = **7%** = ₹28
- 100% = 28 × 100/7 = **₹400**

### Q33. Wholesaler 10% discount orders par (above ₹1000) + 5% extra seasonal discount. ₹20,000 ke order par effective price?
- MF = (9/10)(19/20) = 171/200
- Price = 20,000 × 171/200 = **₹17,100**

### Q34. MP ₹12,000, do successive discounts, doosra 20%, final SP ₹8,400. Pehla discount?
- MF total = 8400/12000 = **7/10**
- Doosra MF = 4/5
- Pehla MF = (7/10) ÷ (4/5) = 7/8
- Pehla discount = 1/8 = **12.5%**

**Note:** Order se farq nahi padta (multiplication hai).

## 18. Relation between CP, MP, SP (Bahut Important – 2025 me bahut aaya)

### Master Relation:

**CP / MP = (100 − Discount%) / (100 + Profit%)**

Ya: **MP / CP = (100 + Profit%) / (100 − Discount%)**

*(Dono ek hi baat hai. Isko apni zubaan par rakho!)*

### Q35. MP ₹800, discount 15%, profit 20%. CP?
- CP/800 = 85/120
- CP = 800 × 85/120 = 1700/3 = **₹566.67**

### Q36. CP ₹500, MP ₹600, discount 20%. Profit/Loss?
- CP/MP = 500/600 = 5/6 = (100 − 20)/(100 + P)
- 5(100 + P) = 6 × 80 = 480 → 100 + P = 96
- P = −4 → **4% loss**

> Sign yaad rakho: minus = loss, plus = profit.

### Q37. SP = 90% of MP, CP = 80% of MP. Profit %?
- MP = 10 units → SP = 9, CP = 8
- Profit = 1/8 × 100 = **12.5%**

### Q38. Vendor 7 shirts khareedta hai 6 shirts ke MP par. 4% discount par bechta hai. Profit %?
- CP : MP = 6 : 7 (7 shirts ka CP = 6 shirts ka MP → per shirt)
- 6/7 = 96/(100 + P)
- 6(100 + P) = 7 × 96 = 672 → 100 + P = 112
- **12% profit**

### Q39. 10 boxes ka CP = 8 boxes ke MP ke barabar, 2% discount. Profit %?
- CP : MP = 8 : 10 = 4 : 5
- 4/5 = 98/(100 + P) → 4(100 + P) = 490 → 100 + P = 122.5
- **22.5% profit**

### Q40. Retailer P par beca (20% profit). Fir wahi phone 1.5P mark kiya aur 10% discount diya. Profit %?
- 20% profit → 6 units = P, CP = 5 units
- Naya SP = 1.5P × 0.9 = 1.35P = (1.35 × 6) = 8.1 units
- Profit = (8.1 − 5)/5 = 3.1/5 = **62%**

## 19. Free Items / Buy-Get Schemes

**Formula (bahut fast):**

**Discount % = (Free items / Total items) × 100**

*(Total items = jo kharide + jo free mile)*

### Q41. 2 shirts ₹1200 each, 3rd free. Effective price per shirt?
- Paid: 2 × 1200 = 2400, mile 3 shirts
- Effective = 2400/3 = **₹800 per shirt**

### Q42. Kaunsi scheme best?
- **Buy 6 Get 4 free:** 4/(6+4) = 4/10 = **40%**
- **Buy 5 Get 5 free:** 5/(5+5) = 5/10 = **50%**
- **Successive 18% & 24%:** 18 + 24 − (18×24)/100 = 42 − 4.32 = **37.68%**
- **Maximum = 50% (Scheme B)**

### Q43. Buy 3 shirts for the price of 2. Effective discount?
- Free = 1, total = 3 → **33.33% discount**

### Q44. Buy 4 books, 5th book 50% off. Effective discount?
- 5th book half free → free = 0.5, total = 5
- 0.5/5 × 100 = **10%**

## 20. Markup + Successive Discount (CP, SP nikalna)

**Core logic:**
- Markup → **CP : MP** ratio
- Discount → **MP : SP** ratio
- Dono multiply karo → **CP : SP** ratio direct milta hai (MP cancel ho jata hai)

### Q45. 60% markup, do successive discounts (25%, 10%), final SP ₹2430. CP?
- Markup MF = 8/5
- Discount MF = (3/4)(9/10) = 27/40
- Net = (8/5)(27/40) = 216/200 = **27/25** (profit 8%)
- CP = 2430 × 25/27 = **₹2250**

### Q46. 50% markup, 10% & 20% discounts, profit ₹80. Selling price?
- MF = (3/2)(9/10)(4/5) = 108/100 → CP : SP = 100 : 108
- 8 units (profit) = ₹80 → 1 unit = ₹10
- SP = 108 × 10 = **₹1,080**

### Q47. 60% markup, 25% & 10% discounts, profit ₹160. CP?
- MF = (8/5)(3/4)(9/10) = 108/100 → profit 8%
- 8 units = 160 → 1 unit = 20
- CP = 100 × 20 = **₹2000**

## 22. Common Mistakes

1. **CP nikaal ke chhod dena** jab SP puchha ho (Q9 wala trap).
2. **Profit/Loss % ko SP par nikalna** — hamesha CP par.
3. **Total quantity galat jagah** (CP ya SP form check na karna).
4. **Dishonest shopkeeper me weight numerator me rakhna** — actual weight denominator me jaata hai.
5. **Free items me denominator galat** — total = paid + free dono.
6. **Sign galti** — loss ko minus aur profit ko plus se hi calculate karo.
7. **Question ko jaldi padhna** — "SP of watch" vs "CP of watch" jaise words.
8. **Option elimination bhoolna** — loss wale options pehle hata do, % approximate lagao.

# Partnership – Complete Notes

## 1. What Is Partnership?

Partnership is a type of business in which two or more people invest money and share the profit or loss.

Each partner's share depends mainly on:

1. The amount of money invested (Capital).
2. The time for which the money is invested.

### Main Formula

**Profit Share = Investment × Time**

For multiple partners:

**Profit Ratio = Capital × Investment Time**

More precisely:

**Profit Ratio = (Capital₁ × Time₁) : (Capital₂ × Time₂) : ...**

This is the most important formula in partnership questions.

---

## 2. Case 1: All Partners Invest for the Same Time

When all partners invest money for the same duration, their profit ratio is equal to their investment ratio.

### Formula

If time is the same:

**Profit Ratio = Capital Ratio**

### Example

A invests ₹20,000.
B invests ₹30,000.

Both invest for one year.

Capital ratio:

20,000 : 30,000

Divide both sides by 10,000:

**A : B = 2 : 3**

Therefore, their profit ratio is also:

**2 : 3**

If the total profit is ₹25,000:

Total ratio parts = 2 + 3 = 5

A's share:

= ₹25,000 × 2/5

= ₹10,000

B's share:

= ₹25,000 × 3/5

= ₹15,000

**Answer: A gets ₹10,000 and B gets ₹15,000.**

### Remember

If the investment time is the same, you do not need to multiply by time again.

---

## 3. Case 2: Partners Invest for Different Times

When partners invest for different durations, multiply each person's capital by their investment time.

### Formula

**Profit Ratio = Capital₁ × Time₁ : Capital₂ × Time₂**

### Example

A invests ₹20,000 for 6 months.
B invests ₹30,000 for 4 months.

A's capital-time value:

20,000 × 6 = 120,000

B's capital-time value:

30,000 × 4 = 120,000

Profit ratio:

120,000 : 120,000

**A : B = 1 : 1**

Both receive equal profit.

### Another Example

A invests ₹10,000 for 12 months.
B invests ₹20,000 for 6 months.

A's capital-time value:

10,000 × 12 = 120,000

B's capital-time value:

20,000 × 6 = 120,000

Profit ratio:

**1 : 1**

Even though B invested twice as much money, A invested for twice as long.

Therefore, their profit shares are equal.

---

## 4. How to Calculate Each Partner's Profit Share

When the total profit is given, use the ratio.

### Formula

If the profit ratio is:

A : B : C = a : b : c

Then:

Total Parts = a + b + c

A's Share = Total Profit × a / (a + b + c)

B's Share = Total Profit × b / (a + b + c)

C's Share = Total Profit × c / (a + b + c)

### Example

The profit ratio of A, B, and C is 2 : 3 : 5.

Total profit = ₹50,000.

Total parts:

2 + 3 + 5 = 10

A's share:

₹50,000 × 2/10 = ₹10,000

B's share:

₹50,000 × 3/10 = ₹15,000

C's share:

₹50,000 × 5/10 = ₹25,000

**Answer: ₹10,000, ₹15,000, and ₹25,000 respectively.**

### Important Rule

The sum of all partners' profit shares must equal the total profit.

---

## 5. Example: Three Partners with Different Investments and Times

A invests ₹4,000 for 3 years.
B invests ₹6,000 for 2 years.
C invests ₹8,000 for 4 years.

The total profit is ₹56,000. Find B's share.

### Step 1: Calculate Capital × Time

A = 4,000 × 3 = 12,000

B = 6,000 × 2 = 12,000

C = 8,000 × 4 = 32,000

### Step 2: Find the Ratio

12,000 : 12,000 : 32,000

Divide by 4,000:

**3 : 3 : 8**

### Step 3: Find Total Parts

3 + 3 + 8 = 14

### Step 4: Calculate B's Share

B's share:

= ₹56,000 × 3/14

= ₹12,000

**Answer: ₹12,000**

---

## 6. When the Investment Ratio Is Given but Time Is Not Given

If the question gives only the investment ratio and does not mention different investment periods, use the investment ratio as the profit ratio, assuming all partners invest for the same duration.

### Example

A invests seven times as much as B.

Investment ratio:

A : B = 7 : 1

Total profit = ₹2,75,000.

Total parts:

7 + 1 = 8

A's share:

= ₹2,75,000 × 7/8

= ₹2,40,625

**Answer: ₹2,40,625**

### Remember

When the investment duration is equal:

**Investment Ratio = Profit Ratio**

---

## 7. Finding the Investment Ratio When Time and Profit Ratios Are Given

Sometimes the question gives:
- The time ratio.
- The profit ratio.
- And asks for the investment ratio.

Use:

**Investment = Profit Share / Time**

For ratios, divide each profit ratio term by its corresponding time ratio term.

### Formula

If:

Time ratio = a : b : c

Profit ratio = x : y : z

Then:

Investment ratio = x/a : y/b : z/c

To avoid fractions, multiply through by a common denominator.

### Example

Time ratio:

A : B : C = 3 : 2 : 7

Profit ratio:

A : B : C = 4 : 3 : 14

Investment ratio:

= 4/3 : 3/2 : 14/7

= 4/3 : 3/2 : 2

Multiply all terms by 6:

= 8 : 9 : 12

**Answer: 8 : 9 : 12**

### Cross-Multiplication Shortcut

For three partners:

Investment ratio is proportional to:

- A: A's profit ratio × B's time × C's time
- B: B's profit ratio × A's time × C's time
- C: C's profit ratio × A's time × B's time

For the example:

A = 4 × 2 × 7 = 56

B = 3 × 3 × 7 = 63

C = 14 × 3 × 2 = 84

Ratio:

56 : 63 : 84

Divide by 7:

**8 : 9 : 12**

---

## 8. When a Partner Joins Later

If a partner joins after some months, they invest for fewer months than the original partners.

### Formula

**Profit Share = Capital × Number of Months Invested**

### Example

A invests ₹45,000 at the beginning of the year.

B invests ₹30,000 and joins later.

At the end of the year, their profit ratio is 2 : 1.

Find when B joined.

### Step 1: Set Up the Ratio

A invests for 12 months.

B invests for x months.

Therefore:

A's capital-time value = 45,000 × 12

B's capital-time value = 30,000 × x

Profit ratio:

(45,000 × 12) : (30,000 × x) = 2 : 1

### Step 2: Simplify

540,000 : 30,000x = 2 : 1

Divide the left terms by 30,000:

18 : x = 2 : 1

Therefore:

18/x = 2/1

18 = 2x

x = 9 months

B invested for 9 months.

### Step 3: Find When B Joined

A full year = 12 months.

B invested for 9 months.

Joining time = 12 − 9 = 3 months after the beginning.

**Answer: B joined after 3 months.**

### Important Difference

- "How many months did B invest?" → 9 months.
- "After how many months did B join?" → 3 months.

---

## 9. When a Partner Joins and Another Partner Withdraws

In these questions, calculate how long each person's capital remained in the business.

### Example

A invests ₹25,000 for one year.
B invests ₹50,000 for one year.
After 7 months, C joins with ₹30,000.
After 9 months, A withdraws the entire investment.

Find C's share if the total profit is ₹3,900.

### Step 1: Simplify the Capital Ratio

Capital ratio:

25,000 : 50,000 : 30,000

Divide by 5,000:

**5 : 10 : 6**

### Step 2: Find Investment Time

A withdraws after 9 months.

A's investment time = 9 months.

B remains for the full year.

B's investment time = 12 months.

C joins after 7 months.

C's investment time = 12 − 7 = 5 months.

### Step 3: Multiply Capital by Time

A = 5 × 9 = 45

B = 10 × 12 = 120

C = 6 × 5 = 30

Profit ratio:

45 : 120 : 30

Divide by 15:

**3 : 8 : 2**

### Step 4: Find C's Share

Total parts:

3 + 8 + 2 = 13

C's share:

= ₹3,900 × 2/13

= ₹600

**Answer: ₹600**

### Remember

- A partner who withdraws money stops earning profit on the withdrawn capital from that point onward.
- A partner who joins later earns profit only for the remaining period.
- If only part of the capital is withdrawn, calculate the two time periods separately.

---

## 10. When Partners Leave at Different Times

### Example

A, B, and C invest in the ratio:

A : B : C = 5 : 7 : 10

C leaves after 3 months.

B leaves 7 months after C leaves.

A remains for the entire year.

Find the ratio of their profit shares.

### Step 1: Find the Investment Durations

C invests for 3 months.

B leaves 7 months after C leaves.

B invests for:

3 + 7 = 10 months

A invests for:

12 months

### Step 2: Multiply Capital by Time

A = 5 × 12 = 60

B = 7 × 10 = 70

C = 10 × 3 = 30

Profit ratio:

60 : 70 : 30

Divide by 10:

**A : B : C = 6 : 7 : 3**

### Step 3: Find A and B's Combined Share

A + B = 6 + 7 = 13 parts

Total parts:

6 + 7 + 3 = 16 parts

Therefore, A and B together receive:

13/16 of the total profit.

**Important:** If a question asks for the combined share of A and B, add their ratio parts before calculating the amount.

---

## 11. When Capital Is Increased After Some Months

If a partner adds more capital, divide the investment into separate periods.

### Example

A initially invests ₹30,000.
After 5 months, A increases the capital by 50%.

Find A's total capital-time value for the year.

### Step 1: First 5 Months

Initial capital = ₹30,000

Capital-time value:

30,000 × 5 = 150,000

### Step 2: Calculate the New Capital

50% of ₹30,000:

= ₹15,000

New capital:

30,000 + 15,000 = ₹45,000

### Step 3: Remaining 7 Months

Capital-time value:

45,000 × 7 = 315,000

### Step 4: Add Both Periods

Total capital-time value:

150,000 + 315,000 = 465,000

**Answer: 465,000 capital-month units.**

### Important Rule

When capital changes:

**Total Capital-Time Value = Sum of (Capital × Time) for Each Period**

---

## 12. When Capital Is Reduced or Partially Withdrawn

If a partner withdraws a percentage of their capital, calculate the remaining capital and use it for the remaining months.

### Example

B initially invests ₹50,000.

After 5 months, B withdraws 60% of the capital.

Find B's capital-time value for the year.

### Step 1: First 5 Months

Capital = ₹50,000

Capital-time value:

50,000 × 5 = 250,000

### Step 2: Calculate the Withdrawal

60% of ₹50,000:

= ₹30,000

Remaining capital:

50,000 − 30,000 = ₹20,000

### Step 3: Remaining 7 Months

Capital-time value:

20,000 × 7 = 140,000

### Step 4: Add Both Periods

Total capital-time value:

250,000 + 140,000 = 390,000

**Answer: 390,000 capital-month units.**

### Remember

- Increase capital → Add the increase.
- Withdraw capital → Subtract the withdrawal.
- Calculate each capital amount for the time it remained invested.

---

## 13. When One Partner Increases Capital and Another Withdraws Capital

These questions combine both concepts.

### Example

A and B initially invest in the ratio:

A : B = 30 : 50

After 5 months:
- A increases their capital by 50%.
- B withdraws 60% of their capital.

Find the profit ratio at the end of the year.

### Step 1: First 5 Months

A's capital = 30

B's capital = 50

A's capital-time value:

30 × 5 = 150

B's capital-time value:

50 × 5 = 250

### Step 2: Calculate the New Capitals

A increases capital by 50%:

30 + 15 = 45

B withdraws 60%:

50 − 30 = 20

### Step 3: Calculate the Remaining 7 Months

A's capital-time value:

45 × 7 = 315

B's capital-time value:

20 × 7 = 140

### Step 4: Add the Values

A's total:

150 + 315 = 465

B's total:

250 + 140 = 390

Profit ratio:

465 : 390

Divide by 15:

**A : B = 31 : 26**

### Important Note

This example uses the capital ratios as relative values. Actual rupee investments are not necessary when only the final ratio is required.

---

## 14. How to Calculate a Percentage Increase or Decrease in Capital

### Capital Increased by p%

New Capital:

= Original Capital × (100 + p)/100

### Capital Decreased by p%

Remaining Capital:

= Original Capital × (100 − p)/100

### Example 1: Increase by 50%

Original capital = ₹30,000

New capital:

= 30,000 × 150/100

= ₹45,000

### Example 2: Decrease by 60%

Original capital = ₹50,000

Remaining capital:

= 50,000 × 40/100

= ₹20,000

### Shortcut

For percentage-based capital changes, using 100 or another convenient multiple can make the arithmetic easier when only a ratio is required.

## 15. Solving Partnership Questions Quickly

Follow this method in most questions.

### Step 1: Write the Partners

For example:

A, B, C

### Step 2: Write Their Capitals

Use the given amounts or simplify them to a ratio if possible.

### Step 3: Find the Investment Time

For each partner, ask:
- When did the partner join?
- When did the partner leave?
- Did the partner increase capital?
- Did the partner withdraw capital?

### Step 4: Calculate Capital × Time

For a constant investment:

Capital × Months

For a changing investment:

Add the capital-time values for all periods.

### Step 5: Simplify the Profit Ratio

Divide by common factors.

### Step 6: Calculate the Required Share

If the total profit is given:

Partner's Share = Total Profit × Partner's Ratio Part / Total Ratio Parts

### Step 7: Read the Question Carefully

It may ask for:
- A partner's share
- The combined share of two partners
- The difference between two shares
- The time when a partner joined
- The investment ratio

These require different final calculations.

---

## 16. Common Mistakes

1. Using only the investment ratio when the partners invest for different durations.
2. Forgetting to multiply capital by time.
3. Counting a partner's investment time incorrectly.
4. Confusing "joined after 3 months" with "invested for 3 months."
5. Forgetting that a partner who joins after 7 months invests for only 5 months in a 12-month year.
6. Forgetting that a partner who leaves after 9 months has invested for 9 months.
7. Applying a percentage increase to the wrong capital amount.
8. Subtracting the wrong percentage when capital is withdrawn.
9. Ignoring the capital-time value of the earlier period when capital changes.
10. Calculating an individual share when the question asks for the combined share.
11. Forgetting to add all the ratio parts before dividing the total profit.
12. Mixing months and years without converting to the same unit.

## 19. Final Revision Sheet

| Situation | Rule |
|---|---|
| Same investment time | Profit ratio = Capital ratio |
| Different investment times | Profit ratio = Capital × Time |
| Total profit given | Share = Total Profit × Ratio Part / Total Parts |
| Partner joins later | Time invested = Total period − Months before joining |
| Partner leaves | Count only the months the capital remained invested |
| Capital increases | Use the new capital for the remaining period |
| Capital decreases | Use the remaining capital for the remaining period |
| Capital changes more than once | Add Capital × Time for every period |
| Combined profit share | Add the required partners' ratio parts |
| Difference in shares | Subtract the relevant ratio parts |

## 20. Most Important Formula

**Profit Ratio = Capital × Time**

For changing capital:

**Total Capital-Time Value = (Capital₁ × Time₁) + (Capital₂ × Time₂) + ...**

For an individual partner's share:

**Partner's Share = Total Profit × Partner's Capital-Time Value / Sum of All Partners' Capital-Time Values**

Remember these formulas and carefully track the number of months each partner's money remains invested. Most partnership questions in this lecture can be solved using these principles.

## 2. Golden Formula (Sabse Important)

**Profit Share ∝ Money × Time**

**Kyun?** Sirf paisa kitna lagaya wo kaafi nahi, **kitne time ke liye** lagaya wo bhi matter karta hai (SIP ki tarah: jitna long-term invest, utna zyada fayda).

**Example:** A ne ₹100, 3 saal ke liye; B ne ₹200, 1 saal ke liye.
- A: 100 × 3 = 300
- B: 200 × 1 = 200
- **Profit ratio = 3 : 2** (A ka hissa zyada, bhale hi B ne paisa zyada lagaya)

> Is formula par pura chapter based hai.

## 3. Profit ko Ratio me Distribute Karna

### Method 1 (Unit method)
**₹500 ko A aur B me 2 : 3 ke ratio me baantna hai.**
- Total ratio = 2 + 3 = 5 units = ₹500
- 1 unit = ₹100
- A = 2 × 100 = **₹200**, B = 3 × 100 = **₹300**

### Method 2 (Fraction method)
- A = 2/5 × 500 = **200**
- B = 3/5 × 500 = **300**

**Rule:** Jiska share chahiye, uska ratio-part upar, total ratio neeche, aur total amount se multiply.

### 3 logon ka example
**₹2700 ko A : B : C = 2 : 3 : 4 me baantna hai.**
- Total ratio = 9 units = ₹2700 → 1 unit = ₹300
- A = 2 × 300 = **₹600**
- B = 3 × 300 = **₹900**
- C = 4 × 300 = **₹1200**

## 4. Basic Questions

### Q1: A ne ₹200, 5 saal ke liye. B ne ₹600, 3 saal ke liye. Profit ratio?
- A = 200 × 5 = 1000
- B = 600 × 3 = 1800
- Ratio = 1000 : 1800 = **5 : 9**

### Q2: A ne ₹500, 2 saal ke liye. B ne ₹1500, 3 saal ke liye. Total profit ₹4400. Dono ka share?
- A = 500 × 2 = 1000
- B = 1500 × 3 = 4500
- Ratio = 1000 : 4500 = **2 : 9** (total 11)
- A = 2/11 × 4400 = **₹800**
- B = 9/11 × 4400 = **₹3600**

### Q3 (Teen partners): A = ₹200 (3 yr), B = ₹300 (4 yr), C = ₹400 (5 yr). Profit ratio?
- A = 200 × 3 = 600
- B = 300 × 4 = 1200
- C = 400 × 5 = 2000
- Ratio = 600 : 1200 : 2000 = **3 : 6 : 10**

## 5. Unknown Value (x ya t) Nikalna

**Idea:** Jahan koi value nahi pata (x), wahan investment × time me **x** likho, aur jo **profit ratio question me diya hai**, uske saath **equate** karo (dono profit ratios hain isliye barabar).

### Q1: A = ₹500 (2 yr), B = ₹x (3 yr). Profit ratio A : B = 2 : 9. Find x.
- A = 500 × 2 = 1000; B = 3x
- 1000 / 3x = 2/9
- 3x × 2 = 9000 → 3x = 4500 → **x = ₹1500**

**Shortcut:** Apne ratio (1000 : 3x) ko diye hue ratio (2 : 9) se divide karo, beech me "=" lagao.
- 1000 ÷ 2 = 500 aur 3x ÷ 9 → 500 = x/3 → x = 1500 ✔

### Q2: A = ₹200 (3 yr), B = ₹300 (t yr), C = ₹400 (5 yr). Profit ratio 3 : 6 : 10. Find t.
- A = 600, B = 300t, C = 2000
- Ratio 600 : 300t : 2000 = 6 : 3t : 20 (÷100)
- Given ratio 3 : 6 : 10 → ×2 → 6 : 12 : 20
- 3t = 12 → **t = 4 years**

**Tip:** Kisi bhi ek partner se equate kar sakte ho (A se, B se, ya C se), answer same aayega.

## 6. Entry Concept (Beech me paisa add karna)

**Rule:** Jitne time tak **purana amount** laga, usko pehle multiply karo. Phir **naya amount** (purana + add kiya) bacha hua time se multiply karo.

**Steps:**
1. Pehle **total business time** dekho (jaise 1 saal = 12 months).
2. Jab paisa add hua, tab tak purana amount laga.
3. Add hone ke baad naya total amount bacha hua time ke liye.
4. Jiska time nahi diya wo **poore time** ke liye maana jayega (understood).

### Q1: A = ₹1500, B = ₹2000. 4 mahine baad A ne ₹500 aur add kiya. Total business 1 saal. Profit ratio?
- Total time = 12 months
- A: 1500 × 4 + (1500 + 500) × 8 = 6000 + 16000 = **22000**
- B (time nahi diya → poora 12 months): 2000 × 12 = **24000**
- Ratio = 22000 : 24000 = **11 : 12**

**Analogy (Sir ki):** Dost 3 ghante baad aaye to shuru ke 3 ghante aapne akele jhele. Waise hi naya paisa aane se pehle purana paisa hi kaam karta hai.

### Q2: A = ₹2500, B = ₹3500. 3 mahine baad A ne ₹500 add kiya. Uske **3 mahine aur baad** B ne ₹500 add kiya. Total 1 saal. Profit ratio?
- Total time = 12 months
- **A:** 3 months ke liye 2500, baaki 9 months ke liye (2500 + 500 = 3000)
  → 2500 × 3 + 3000 × 9 = 7500 + 27000 = **34500**
- **B:** "3 months more" matlab **3 + 3 = 6 months** baad aaya.
  6 months ke liye 3500, baaki 6 months ke liye (3500 + 500 = 4000)
  → 3500 × 6 + 4000 × 6 = 21000 + 24000 = **45000**
- Ratio = 34500 : 45000 = **23 : 30**

## 7. Withdrawal Concept (Beech me paisa nikalna)

**Rule:** Entry ke ulta: nikalne ke baad **amount kam** ho jata hai.

### Q: A = ₹4000, B = ₹3000. Total business 18 months. 8 months baad A ne ₹1000 nikaal liye. 12 months baad B ne ₹1000 add kiye. Profit ratio?
- **A:** 4000 × 8 + (4000 − 1000) × 10 = 32000 + 30000 = **62000**
- **B:** 3000 × 12 + (3000 + 1000) × 6 = 36000 + 18000... (4000 × 6 = 24000) → 36000 + 24000 = **60000**
- Ratio = 62000 : 60000 = **31 : 30**

**Note:** Yahan total time 12 nahi, **18 months** hai. Isliye bacha hua time = 18 − 8 = 10, aur 18 − 12 = 6.

---

## 8. "After x more months" ka Matlab

- **"After 3 months, A added. After 3 more months, B added"** → B **6 months** (3 + 3) baad aaya (cumulative).
- Sirf **"after 12 months"** likha hai (more nahi) → seedha **12 months** baad.

> **"more"** word ka matlab = pichle time me aur jodo.

---

## 9. "Garib Banao" Approach (Shortcut)

**Idea:** Investment ko **chhota** kar do, taaki calculation easy ho.

**Rules:**
1. Sabke investment se **common zeros / common factor** hata do (jaise sabme 3 zero hai to 3 hata do; agar kisi me kam hain to jitne sabme common ho utne hi).
2. **Sirf investment ke saath ye karte hain.** **Profit ke saath kabhi nahi!** (₹1200 profit ke zero hataoge to ₹12 ho jayega, galat!)

**Example:** A = 4000, B = 3000, Add/withdraw = 1000
→ Garib banao (3 zero hata do): A = 4, B = 3, change = 1
- A: 4 × 8 + 3 × 10 = 32 + 30 = 62
- B: 3 × 12 + 4 × 6 = 36 + 24 = 60
- Ratio = 31 : 30 ✔ (same answer)

---

## 10. "x months before" Ka Confusion

**Rule:** Agar partner **business khatam hone se x months pehle** chhod de, to uska time = **Total − x**.

**Examples:** Business 12 months ka:
- 8 months pehle chhoda → time = 12 − 8 = **4 months**
- 9 months pehle chhoda → time = 12 − 9 = **3 months**
- x months pehle chhoda → time = **12 − x**

**Aur agar "x months baad aaya"** → time = Total − x.

## 11. "Out of" Type (Fav. in exams ⭐)

### Q1: A aur B ne 3 : 5 ratio me invest kiya. 6 months baad C ne B ke investment ka 3 guna lekar join kiya. Saal ke end me total profit ₹6200. A ka share?
- Total time = 12 months
- A = 3 (12 months), B = 5 (12 months), C = 15 (kyunki 3 × B = 15) (6 months)
- A = 3 × 12 = 36; B = 5 × 12 = 60; C = 15 × 6 = 90
- Ratio = 36 : 60 : 90 = **6 : 10 : 15** (total 31)
- A's share = 6/31 × 6200 = 6 × 200 = **₹1200**

### Q2 (x months ka): A = ₹12,000, B = ₹12,000 (sirf x months ke liye), C = ₹8,000 (jisne saal khatam hone se x months pehle chhod diya). Total annual profit ₹3200 me se A ko ₹1800 mile. x = ?

**Step 1 (Garib banao, 4000 se divide):** A = 3, B = 3, C = 2 (thousand units ... ya seedha ratio banao)
- A = 12 × 12 = 144
- B = 12 × x = 12x
- C = 8 × (12 − x) [x months pehle chhodne par time = 12 − x]
- Sabko 4 se divide: **36 : 3x : 2(12 − x)**

**Step 2:** A ko ₹1800 → baaki (B + C) ko ₹1400
- **A : (B + C) = 1800 : 1400 = 9 : 7**

**Step 3:** Equation
- 36 / [3x + 2(12 − x)] = 9/7
- 3x + 24 − 2x = x + 24
- 36 / (x + 24) = 9/7
- 36 × 7 = 9(x + 24)
- 28 = x + 24 → **x = 4 months**

> **Tip:** "Out of total profit" wale questions ko pehle practice kar lena. Ye bahut baar aate hain.

## 12. Increase / Decrease by % Wale Questions

### Q: Rahul ne ₹x, Vikas ne ₹7000 invest kiye. 5 months baad Rahul ne apna investment **50%** badha diya. Vikas ne **7 months** baad apna investment **30%** ghata diya. Total business 12 months. Rahul ka share ₹1550, total profit ₹3020. x = ?

**Step 1: Rahul**
- 5 months ke liye x
- Baaki 7 months ke liye x × 3/2 = **3x/2** (50% increase = 3/2 se multiply)
- Rahul = 5x + 7 × (3x/2) = 5x + 21x/2 = **31x/2**

**Step 2: Vikas**
- 7 months ke liye ₹7000
- Baaki 5 months ke liye 7000 − 30% of 7000 = 7000 − 2100 = **₹4900**
- Vikas = 7000 × 7 + 4900 × 5 = 49000 + 24500 = **73500**

**Step 3: Profit split**
- Total profit = 3020; Rahul = 1550 → Vikas = 3020 − 1550 = **1470**
- Profit ratio = 1550 : 1470 = **155 : 147**

**Step 4: Equate**
- (31x/2) : 73500 = 155 : 147
- 31x : 147000 = 155 : 147 (dono side ×2)
- 31x ÷ 155 = 147000 ÷ 147 → 31x/155 = 1000 → 31x = 155000
- **x = ₹5000**

**Conversion yaad rakho:**
- x% increase → multiply (100 + x)/100 (50% → 3/2)
- x% decrease → multiply (100 − x)/100 (30% → 7/10)

## 13. Difference of Profit Shares

### Q: A, B, C ka investment ratio 12 : 5 : 7 (₹48,000 : ₹20,000 : ₹28,000 ko 4000 se divide karke), time sabka same (1 saal). B ka profit ₹3500. A aur C ke profit me difference?

- Time same hai → ignore (12/12 cancel)
- Profit ratio = 12 : 5 : 7
- B = 5 units = ₹3500 → 1 unit = ₹700
- A − C = 12 − 7 = **5 units** = 5 × 700 = **₹3500**
  (Pehle se B ke 5 units diye the, to calculation karne ki zarurat hi nahi thi, 5 ratio = 3500.)

> **Lesson:** Jab time sabka same ho, to time likhne ki zarurat nahi (sirf investment ratio use karo).

## 14. Quick Revision Cheat-Sheet

| Topic | Key Trick |
|---|---|
| Profit share | Money × Time |
| Same time | Sirf investment ka ratio |
| Same money | Sirf time ka ratio |
| Distribution | Unit method / fraction method |
| Unknown x | Profit ratio se equate (diye hue ratio se) |
| Beech me add | Purana × pehle time + Naya × baaki time |
| Beech me nikala | Ghata hua amount × baaki time |
| "After x more months" | Cumulative (3 + 3 = 6) |
| "x months before end" | Time = Total − x |
| Garib banao | Sirf investment se common zeros hatao, profit se nahi |
| Time nahi diya | Poora business time maano |
| x% increase | × (100 + x)/100 |
| x% decrease | × (100 − x)/100 |
| "Out of total profit" | A : (B + C) ka ratio banao, equation solve |

## 15. Common Mistakes

1. **Time ko ignore karna** — sirf paisa dekhna (A ki share kam hai ya zyada, time se decide hota hai).
2. **Profit ke zero bhi hata dena** (Garib banao approach me) — sirf investment ke!
3. **"After 3 more months" ko 3 months samajhna** — wo cumulative (6) hota hai.
4. **Total time 12 le lena jab question me 18 months diye ho** — dhyan se padho.
5. **Time nahi diya to ignore karna** — iska matlab poora business time.
6. **x months before me time = x lena** — time = Total − x.
7. **Add/withdraw ke baad purana amount hi use karna** — naya amount (add/minus ke baad) use karo.
8. **% increase me sirf % ko jodna** (jaise 50% increase = x + 50) — multiply (3/2) karo.

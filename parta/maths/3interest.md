# Simple Interest and Compound Interest (SI & CI) — Complete Notes

## 1. Basic Terminology

### Principal (P)
The money originally borrowed, lent, or invested is called the **Principal**.

Hindi: मूलधन

Example: If you borrow ₹1,000, then Principal = ₹1,000.

### Interest (I)
The extra money paid or earned on the principal is called **Interest**.

Hindi: ब्याज

Example: You borrow ₹1,000 and return ₹1,100.

Interest = ₹1,100 − ₹1,000 = ₹100

### Amount (A)
The total money returned after adding interest to the principal is called the **Amount**.

Hindi: मिश्रधन

**Formula:**

Amount = Principal + Interest

A = P + I

Therefore:

- P = A − I
- I = A − P

### Rate of Interest (R)
The percentage of interest charged per year is called the Rate of Interest.

Example: A rate of 10% per annum means interest is calculated at 10% per year.

### Time (T)
The period for which the money is borrowed or invested.

Examples: 2 years, 5 years, 10 years.

---

## 2. Types of Interest

There are two main types of interest:

1. Simple Interest (SI) — साधारण ब्याज
2. Compound Interest (CI) — चक्रवृद्धि ब्याज

### Simple Interest (SI)

Simple Interest is calculated only on the original principal every year.

The principal remains unchanged for the calculation of interest.

Example:

Principal = ₹1,000  
Rate = 10% per year

| Year | Principal | Interest for that year |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | ₹1,000 | ₹100 |
| 2 | ₹1,000 | ₹100 |
| 3 | ₹1,000 | ₹100 |

Total SI = ₹100 + ₹100 + ₹100 = ₹300

### Compound Interest (CI)

Compound Interest is calculated on the principal **and the interest already added** to it.

This means interest can earn further interest.

Example:

Principal = ₹1,000  
Rate = 10% per year

| Year | Starting Amount | Interest | Ending Amount |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | ₹1,000 | ₹100 | ₹1,100 |
| 2 | ₹1,100 | ₹110 | ₹1,210 |
| 3 | ₹1,210 | ₹121 | ₹1,331 |

After 3 years:

- SI = ₹300
- CI = ₹331

The difference occurs because, under CI, interest is added to the amount and earns interest in later years.

---

## 3. Simple Interest Formula

The most important formula is:

**SI = (P × R × T) / 100**

Where:

- P = Principal
- R = Annual rate of interest
- T = Time in years

Other useful formulas:

**Principal:**

P = (SI × 100) / (R × T)

**Rate:**

R = (SI × 100) / (P × T)

**Time:**

T = (SI × 100) / (P × R)

**Amount:**

A = P + SI

### Example 1: Find Simple Interest

Principal = ₹1,000  
Rate = 10% per annum  
Time = 3 years

SI = (1,000 × 10 × 3) / 100

SI = ₹300

Amount = ₹1,000 + ₹300 = **₹1,300**

### Example 2: A Larger Calculation

Principal = ₹5,000  
Rate = 20% per annum  
Time = 4 years

SI = (5,000 × 20 × 4) / 100

SI = **₹4,000**

Amount = ₹5,000 + ₹4,000 = ₹9,000

### Example 3: Fractional Rate

Principal = ₹1,200  
Rate = 16⅔% per annum  
Time = 10 years

Convert the rate into a fraction:

16⅔% = 50/3% = 1/6

Therefore, the annual interest is 1/6 of the principal.

Annual interest = ₹1,200 ÷ 6 = ₹200

SI for 10 years = ₹200 × 10

**SI = ₹2,000**

---

## 4. Shortcut: Calculate SI Mentally

Under SI, the interest earned each year remains the same if the principal and rate remain unchanged.

### Example

Principal = ₹5,000  
Rate = 20%  
Time = 4 years

20% of ₹5,000 = ₹1,000 per year.

Total SI = ₹1,000 × 4 = ₹4,000.

You can also combine the yearly percentages:

20% × 4 years = 80%

SI = 80% of ₹5,000 = ₹4,000.

### General shortcut

For simple interest:

**Total interest percentage = R × T**

Here, R is the annual rate expressed as a percentage.

Example: 12% per year for 5 years gives total SI equal to 60% of the principal.

---

## 5. How SI Changes When P or R Changes

### Rule 1: Principal changes

If the rate and time remain constant:

- Principal doubles → SI doubles.
- Principal becomes 3 times → SI becomes 3 times.
- Principal becomes half → SI becomes half.

### Rule 2: Rate changes

If the principal and time remain constant:

- Rate doubles → SI doubles.
- Rate becomes 3 times → SI becomes 3 times.

### Important condition

These shortcuts work when the other relevant values remain unchanged.

---

## 6. Questions Where Principal Changes After Some Years

### Example

The SI for 10 years on a principal is ₹1,500.

After 5 years, the principal becomes 3 times its original value. Find the total SI for 10 years, assuming the rate stays the same.

**Step 1: Find the interest for one year on the original principal.**

₹1,500 ÷ 10 = ₹150

**Step 2: Find SI for the first 5 years.**

₹150 × 5 = ₹750

**Step 3: Find SI for the next 5 years.**

The principal is now 3 times the original principal.

Annual interest = ₹150 × 3 = ₹450

Interest for the next 5 years = ₹450 × 5 = ₹2,250

**Step 4: Add both parts.**

Total SI = ₹750 + ₹2,250

**Answer = ₹3,000**

---

## 7. Amount and Principal in Percentage Form

For percentage-based questions, consider the principal as **100%**.

Therefore:

- Principal = 100%
- SI = the interest percentage accumulated over the given time
- Amount = 100% + SI percentage

### Example

Rate = 10% per year  
Time = 10 years

Under SI:

Total interest percentage = 10 × 10 = 100%

So:

- Principal = 100%
- SI = 100%
- Amount = 200%

Therefore:

**Amount : Principal = 200 : 100 = 2 : 1**

This shortcut is useful when the question asks for a ratio rather than the actual rupee amount.

---

## 8. When an Amount Becomes 2 Times, 3 Times, or 5 Times

Words such as **becomes**, **doubles**, and **triples** generally refer to the final amount in these questions.

For example:

If the principal is ₹100 and the amount becomes ₹300:

SI = ₹300 − ₹100 = ₹200

Do not mistake the final amount for the interest.

### Example: An amount triples in 4 years

Suppose the principal is ₹1.

After 4 years, the amount becomes ₹3.

Interest = ₹3 − ₹1 = ₹2

SI for 4 years = ₹2

Annual interest = ₹2 ÷ 4 = ₹0.50

Rate = (0.50 ÷ 1) × 100

**Rate = 50% per annum**

### Example: An amount doubles in 6 years

Principal = ₹1  
Amount after 6 years = ₹2

Interest = ₹2 − ₹1 = ₹1

The principal earns ₹1 as interest in 6 years.

To earn ₹4 as interest under SI, the time required is:

6 × 4 = **24 years**

Therefore, the amount becomes 5 times the principal in 24 years.

---

## 9. Finding Principal When Amounts Are Given for Different Times

**Important rule:** The amount and interest used in subtraction must refer to the same time period.

### Example

An amount is ₹600 after 1 year and ₹900 after 4 years. Find the principal and annual rate.

**Step 1: Find the increase in amount.**

₹900 − ₹600 = ₹300

This is the SI earned over the extra 3 years.

**Step 2: Find annual SI.**

₹300 ÷ 3 = ₹100 per year

**Step 3: Find principal.**

The amount after 1 year is ₹600.

Principal = Amount − SI for 1 year

P = ₹600 − ₹100 = ₹500

**Step 4: Find rate.**

R = (100 × 100) ÷ 500

**Rate = 20% per annum**

### Common mistake

Do not subtract one year's interest from an amount that represents four years. First convert the interest to the matching time period.

---

## 10. Compound Interest Formula

When interest is compounded annually:

**Amount:**

A = P × (1 + R/100)^T

**Compound Interest:**

CI = A − P

Therefore:

CI = P × (1 + R/100)^T − P

Where:

- P = Principal
- R = Annual rate
- T = Number of years
- A = Final amount

### Example: CI for 2 years

Principal = ₹1,000  
Rate = 10% per year

**Year 1**

Interest = 10% of ₹1,000 = ₹100

Amount = ₹1,100

**Year 2**

Interest = 10% of ₹1,100 = ₹110

Amount = ₹1,210

CI = ₹1,210 − ₹1,000

**CI = ₹210**

---

## 11. Calculating CI Year by Year

When the annual rate is the same, the amount at the end of each year becomes the starting amount for the next year.

### Example

Principal = ₹6,000  
Rate = 20% per year  
Time = 2 years

**Year 1**

Interest = 20% of ₹6,000 = ₹1,200

Amount = ₹7,200

**Year 2**

Interest = 20% of ₹7,200 = ₹1,440

Amount = ₹8,640

CI = ₹8,640 − ₹6,000

**CI = ₹2,640**

---

## 12. The Effective Interest Percentage

Under SI, the total interest percentage is simply:

R × T

Under CI, the interest percentage is calculated on a growing amount.

### CI for 2 years

If the annual rate is R%, the effective interest percentage for 2 years is:

**Effective rate = R + R + (R × R)/100**

Or:

**Effective rate = 2R + R²/100**

### Example: 10% for 2 years

Effective rate = 10 + 10 + (10 × 10)/100

= 20 + 1

= **21%**

For a principal of ₹6,000:

CI = 21% of ₹6,000

CI = ₹1,260

### Example: 20% for 2 years

Effective rate = 20 + 20 + (20 × 20)/100

= 40 + 4

= **44%**

For a principal of ₹6,000:

CI = 44% of ₹6,000

CI = **₹2,640**

This method is useful when you need only the interest percentage and want to avoid calculating the amount year by year.

---

## 13. Effective Interest for 3 Years

For three years, you can calculate the amount year by year or combine the effective percentages.

If the annual rate is R%, the effective percentage for three years is:

**R + R²/100 + (R + R²/100) × R/100 + R**

A simpler method is to calculate the amount multiplier:

**Amount = P × (1 + R/100)³**

Then:

CI = Amount − P

### Example: 10% for 3 years

Principal = ₹1,000

Year 1 amount = ₹1,100

Year 2 amount = ₹1,210

Year 3 amount = ₹1,331

CI = ₹1,331 − ₹1,000

**CI = ₹331**

Effective interest percentage = **33.1%**

---

## 14. Difference Between CI and SI

For the same principal, annual rate, and time, CI is generally greater than SI when the rate is positive and the period exceeds one year.

### Difference for 2 years

**CI − SI = P × R² / 10,000**

This shortcut applies when the rate is annual and compounding is yearly.

### Example

Principal = ₹10,000  
Rate = 10%  
Time = 2 years

Difference = 10,000 × 10² / 10,000

Difference = **₹100**

Check:

- SI = ₹2,000
- CI = ₹2,100
- Difference = ₹100

### Difference for 3 years

For annual compounding, the difference is:

**CI − SI = P × [3(R/100)² + (R/100)³]**

Equivalent form:

**CI − SI = P × (3R²/10,000 + R³/1,000,000)**

Use the formula carefully and keep the rate and time units consistent.

---

## 15. Finding the Interest for a Particular Year Under CI

Under CI, the interest for each year increases when the rate is positive because the starting amount increases.

### Example

Principal = ₹1,000  
Rate = 20% per year

- First-year interest = ₹200
- Second-year interest = ₹240
- Third-year interest = ₹288

Notice the pattern:

Second-year interest = First-year interest × 1.2

Third-year interest = Second-year interest × 1.2

For a 20% annual rate, multiply by 120/100 = 6/5 to move from one year's interest to the next.

For a general rate R%, multiply by:

**(100 + R)/100**

This shortcut assumes the rate and annual compounding frequency remain unchanged.

---

## 16. Growth Multipliers

Under CI, the amount grows by a fixed multiplier each year.

| Annual rate | Yearly multiplier |
|---|---:|
| 5% | 1.05 |
| 10% | 1.10 |
| 20% | 1.20 |
| 25% | 1.25 |
| 50% | 1.50 |
| 100% | 2.00 |

### Example

At 20% annual CI:

- After 1 year: P × 1.2
- After 2 years: P × 1.2²
- After 3 years: P × 1.2³

This is useful when the question asks how many years are required for an amount to become a particular multiple.

---

## 17. When an Amount Becomes a Multiple Under CI

Suppose an amount becomes 3 times the principal in 10 years.

Using annual compounding:

A = P × (1 + R/100)^T

If A = 3P:

3P = P × (1 + R/100)^10

Cancel P:

3 = (1 + R/100)^10

For questions that give a growth multiple and ask for time, compare the powers of the same multiplier when possible.

### Important distinction

Do not use the SI assumption that equal amounts of interest are added every year. Under CI, the amount grows by multiplication, so the interest amount changes each year.

---

## 18. Quick Formula Sheet

### Simple Interest

- SI = PRT/100
- A = P + SI
- P = A − SI
- R = SI × 100/(P × T)
- T = SI × 100/(P × R)

### Compound Interest

- A = P(1 + R/100)^T
- CI = A − P
- Two-year effective rate = 2R + R²/100
- Two-year difference: CI − SI = PR²/10,000

### Percentage shortcuts

- Principal = 100%
- Amount = Principal + Interest
- Under SI, total interest percentage = R × T
- Under CI, the amount is multiplied by (1 + R/100) each year

---

## 19. Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Confusing Amount with Interest:** Amount includes both principal and interest.
2. **Using the wrong time period:** Amount and interest must refer to the same time period when subtracting.
3. **Treating SI and CI as the same:** SI is calculated on the original principal; CI includes previously added interest.
4. **Forgetting the annual rate:** A rate of 10% per annum is a yearly rate.
5. **Adding yearly CI percentages directly:** For CI, the base changes each year.
6. **Using the two-year CI shortcut for another duration:** The formula PR²/10,000 is for the two-year difference under annual compounding.
7. **Mixing percentage and decimal forms:** If using R/100, do not divide by 100 again unnecessarily.

## 21. Final Revision Strategy

When solving an interest question:

1. Identify the principal, rate, and time.
2. Determine whether the question uses SI or CI.
3. Check whether the question asks for interest, amount, principal, rate, time, a ratio, or a difference.
4. For SI, use PRT/100 or calculate the yearly interest.
5. For CI, calculate the amount year by year or use the compound amount formula.
6. Match the time period before subtracting interest from an amount.
7. Check whether the question asks for a final amount or only the interest.

**Remember:**

- SI: Interest is calculated on the original principal.
- CI: Interest is calculated on the principal plus previously accumulated interest.
- Amount = Principal + Interest.

# PART A – SIMPLE INTEREST

## 3.1 Formula

**SI = (P × R × T) / 100**

Lekin simple cases me formula ki jagah **percentage method** zyada fast hai.

### Percentage Method (Sabse fast)

**Total interest % = Rate × Time** (kyunki har saal same % milta hai)
Phir Principal ka wo % nikalo.

**Examples:**

| P | R | T | Rate × Time | SI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ₹1000 | 10% | 3 yr | 30% | 30% of 1000 = **₹300** |
| ₹5000 | 20% | 4 yr | 80% | 80% of 5000 = **₹4000** |
| ₹1500 | 30% | 5 yr | 150% | 150% of 1500 = **₹2250** |

### Fraction wale rates (percentage chapter ka use!)

Mixed % ko fraction me badlo. Common fractions yaad rakho:
- 16⅔% = **1/6**
- 14²⁄₇% = **1/7**
- 33⅓% = **1/3**
- 6⅔% = **1/15**
- 12.5% = **1/8**

**Ex1:** ₹1200, 16⅔% (=1/6), 10 years
- SI = 1200 × (1/6) × 10 = **₹2000**

**Ex2:** ₹1400, 14²⁄₇% (=1/7), 6 years
- SI = 1400 × (1/7) × 6 = **₹1200**
- **Warning:** Formula me R/100 ki jagah poora "1/7" lagana hai (kyunki 1/7 hi R/100 ki value hai). Dobara /100 mat karna!

---

## 3.2 SI ke Scaling Rules (Important!)

- Agar **P aur T same** hain aur **Rate n guna** hota hai → **SI bhi n guna**
  (₹1000, 10%→20%→30% par SI = 100 → 200 → 300)
- Agar **R aur T same** hain aur **Principal n guna** hota hai → **SI bhi n guna**

### Q: 10 saal ka SI ₹1500 hai. 5 saal baad principal 3 guna ho jata hai. Total SI?
- 1 saal ka SI = 1500/10 = ₹150
- Pehle 5 saal: 150 × 5 = ₹750
- Agle 5 saal (principal ×3): 750 × 3 = ₹2250
- **Total = 750 + 2250 = ₹3000**

---

## 3.3 Amount vs Principal – Keyword Trick

- **"amounts to"**, **"becomes"**, **"grows to"**, **"doubles/triples"** → ye **Amount** ki baat hai
- Sirf **"sum"** ya **"principal"** ya "money lent/invested" → **Principal**

---

## 3.4 "Money Doubles / Triples" Type

**Concept:** Agar P = 1 maan lo to Amount bataya gaya hai, Interest = Amount − P.

### Q1: 10% rate, 10 years, SI = ₹2000. Amount : Principal ratio?
- Interest % = 10 × 10 = **100%** of P
- Amount = P + SI = 100% + 100% = **200%**
- **A : P = 2 : 1**

### Q2: Koi sum 4 saal me 3 guna ho jata hai. Rate?
- P = ₹1 → A = ₹3 → SI = ₹2 (4 saal me)
- 1 saal ka SI = 2/4 = ₹0.5
- Rate = (1 year SI / P) × 100 = 0.5/1 × 100 = **50%**

**Formula:** R = (1 saal ka SI / P) × 100

### Q3: Sum 6 saal me double hota hai. Kitne saal me 5 guna hoga?
- P = 1 → double = 2 → SI = 1 (6 saal me)
- 5 guna → SI = 4
- ₹1 jodne me 6 saal → ₹4 jodne me **4 × 6 = 24 years**

---

## 3.5 Amount se Principal – Same Time Rule (Bahut Important!)

**Rule:** **P = A − SI** tabhi sahi hai jab **dono ka time SAME ho.**

**Galat example:** A (2 saal ka) = ₹5000, SI (1 saal ka) = ₹1000 → P = 4000 ❌
**Sahi:** 1 saal SI = 1000 → 2 saal ka SI = 2000 → P = 5000 − 2000 = **₹3000** ✔

### Q: Koi sum 1 saal me ₹600 aur 4 saal me ₹900 ho jata hai (SI par). Sum aur rate?
- 1 saal → 4 saal = 3 saal me **₹300** jude
- 1 saal ka SI = 300/3 = **₹100**
- **Method 1:** A(1 yr) − SI(1 yr) = 600 − 100 = **₹500** = P
- **Method 2:** A(4 yr) − SI(4 yr) = 900 − 400 = **₹500** ✔
- Rate = (100/500) × 100 = **20%**

### Q: Koi sum 3 saal me ₹14,880 aur 5 saal me ₹16,800 ho jata hai. Sum?
- 3 → 5 saal (2 saal) me jude: 16,800 − 14,880 = **₹1920**
- 1 saal ka SI = 960
- 3 saal ka SI = 960 × 3 = 2880
- P = 14,880 − 2880 = **₹12,000**

---

## 3.6 Alag-alag Rates alag-alag time par

### Q: Ramu ne sum lend ki: 4% pehle 3 saal, 8% agle 4 saal, 12% 7 saal ke baad. 11 saal me SI = ₹27,600. Sum?
- 3 yr × 4% = **12%**
- 4 yr × 8% = **32%**
- Baaki 4 yr × 12% = **48%**
- Total = 12 + 32 + 48 = **92%** of P
- 92% = 27,600 → 1% = 300 → P (100%) = **₹30,000**

---

## 3.7 Ratio / Distribution Questions

### Q: ₹18,000 teen parts me 3%, 4%, 6% par invest kiya (1 saal). Teeno se interest equal mila. 6% wali amount?
- Equal interest → investment **inverse of rate**
- Ratio = 1/3 : 1/4 : 1/6 → LCM 12 se multiply → **4 : 3 : 2**
- (Hide & Seek trick: x chhupao → baaki multiply: 4×6=24, 3×6=18, 2×... jo bhi ho, simplify karke 4:3:2)
- 6% wali (ratio 2) = 18,000 × 2/9 = **₹4000**

---

## 3.8 Age-based Question (Mains Level)

### Q: ₹29,000 do bete (age 9 aur 13) ke liye invest kiya, 4.5% SI par, aise ki 21 saal ke hone par dono ko **equal amount** mile. Chhote ka share?

- Chhota (9 yr): 21 − 9 = **12 years** ka interest
- Bada (13 yr): 21 − 13 = **8 years** ka interest
- Chhote ka interest % = 4.5 × 12 = **54%** → Amount = **154%** of x
- Bade ka interest % = 4.5 × 8 = **36%** → Amount = **136%** of y
- Equal amount: 154% of x = 136% of y
- x : y = 136 : 154 = **68 : 77**
- Chhote ka share = 29000 × 68/145 = 200 × 68 = **₹13,600**

**Lesson:** Time = (jab paisa milega − abhi ki age). Aur "equal **amount**" hai, "equal **interest**" nahi — isliye 100 add karna padta hai.

# PART B – COMPOUND INTEREST

## 4.1 CI ka Concept (Dekho kaise hota hai)

**Ex:** P = ₹1000, R = 10%, T = 2 years
- Year 1 interest: 10% of 1000 = ₹100
- Year 2 interest: 10% of 1000 = ₹100 **+ 10% of pichhla interest (100) = ₹10**
- Total CI = 100 + 100 + 10 = **₹210**

**Ex:** P = ₹2000, R = 20%, T = 2 years
- Year 1: 400
- Year 2: 400 + (20% of 400 = 80)
- CI = 400 + 400 + 80 = **₹880**

---

## 4.2 Slab Method (Sabse popular, mental calc ke liye best)

**Idea:** Har saal ke interest ko "layers" me likho. Pattern **binomial / Pascal's triangle** jaisa chalta hai.

### 2 Years: Pattern **1 : 2 : 1**
- Level 1: Principal ka R% (jo har saal hota hai) → **× 2** (2 saal)
- Level 2: Level 1 ka R% → **× 1**

**Ex:** ₹25,000, 20%, 2 yr
- Level 1: 20% of 25,000 = 5000 → ×2 = **10,000**
- Level 2: 20% of 5000 = 1000 → ×1 = **1000**
- **CI = 11,000** ✔

### 3 Years: Pattern **1 : 3 : 3 : 1**
- Level 1 (P ka R%) → **× 3**
- Level 2 (Level 1 ka R%) → **× 3**
- Level 3 (Level 2 ka R%) → **× 1**

**Ex 1:** ₹8000, 10%, 3 yr
- L1 = 800 → ×3 = 2400
- L2 = 80 → ×3 = 240
- L3 = 8 → ×1 = 8
- **CI = 2400 + 240 + 8 = ₹2648**

**Ex 2:** ₹2160, 16⅔% (1/6), 3 yr
- L1 = 360 → ×3 = 1080
- L2 = 60 → ×3 = 180
- L3 = 10 → ×1 = 10
- **CI = ₹1270**

**Ex 3:** ₹33,750, 6⅔% (1/15), 3 yr
- L1 = 2250 → ×3 = 6750
- L2 = 150 → ×3 = 450
- L3 = 10 → ×1 = 10
- **CI = ₹7210**

> Jab percentage "nice" fraction hota hai (10%, 20%, 1/6, 1/15, 1/3) tab slab method bahut fast hai.

---

## 4.3 Chain Approach

**Idea:** Rate ko fraction banao → **Amount multiplier** = (1 + fraction).
Har saal me P ko isi multiplier se multiply karo.

| Rate | Fraction | Multiplier |
|---|---|---|
| 20% | 1/5 | **6/5** |
| 10% | 1/10 | **11/10** |
| 16⅔% | 1/6 | **7/6** |
| 12.5% | 1/8 | **9/8** |
| 6⅔% | 1/15 | **16/15** |
| 33⅓% | 1/3 | **4/3** |
| 8% | 2/25 | **27/25** |

**Formula:** **Amount = P × (multiplier)^T**, phir **CI = Amount − P**

**Ex 1:** ₹2000, 20%, 2 yr
- A = 2000 × (6/5)² = 2000 × 36/25 = **₹2880**
- CI = 2880 − 2000 = **₹880**

**Ex 2:** ₹2160, 1/6, 3 yr
- A = 2160 × (7/6)³ = 2160 × 343/216 = **₹3430**
- CI = 3430 − 2160 = **₹1270** ✔ (slab se bhi yehi aaya)

---

## 4.4 Successive Approach (Ratio method – Principal : Amount)

**Idea:** Per saal P : A ka ratio likho, jitne saal utni baar multiply karo.

**Ex:** ₹33,750, 6⅔% (1/15), 3 yr
- Ek saal: P : A = 15 : 16
- 3 saal: 15³ : 16³ = **3375 : 4096**
- CI ratio = 4096 − 3375 = **721**
- 3375 units = 33,750 → 1 unit = 10
- CI = 721 × 10 = **₹7210** ✔

**Ex:** ₹8000, 10%, 3 yr
- P : A = 10 : 11 → 10³ : 11³ = **1000 : 1331**
- CI = 331 units; 1000 units = 8000 → 1 unit = 8
- CI = 331 × 8 = **₹2648** ✔

---

## 4.5 Fractional Time (jaise 2¼ years)

**Method:** Poore saalon ka CI lo + bacha hua part ka **simple fraction** (pichhle/agle saal ke interest ka hissa).

### Q: ₹2700, 33⅓% (1/3), 2¼ years. CI?
- Slab (3 yr): L1 = 900, L2 = 300, L3 = 100
- Year 1 CI = 900
- Year 2 CI = 900 + 300 = 1200
- Year 3 CI = 900 + 300 + 300 + 100 = 1600
- 2 saal ka CI = 900 + 1200 = **2100**
- Baaki ¼ saal = **¼ × (3rd year ka interest 1600) = 400**
- **Total CI = 2100 + 400 = ₹2500**

(Check: 2 saal baad Amount = 2700 × 16/9 = 4800. ¼ saal ka SI on 4800 at 33⅓% = 4800 × 1/3 × ¼ = 400 ✔)

---

## 4.6 Specific Year ka CI (Sirf ek saal ka)

**Rule:** Agar kisi saal ka CI pata ho to **agle saal ka CI = us CI × (1 + R/100)** (yaani usi chain multiplier se).

### Ex: ₹1000, 20%, 3 years
- Year 1 CI = 200
- Year 2 CI = 200 × 6/5 = **240**
- Year 3 CI = 240 × 6/5 = **288**
- Total 3 yr CI = 200 + 240 + 288 = **728** ✔

### Q: 2nd year ka CI = ₹200, 3rd year ka CI = ₹210. Rate?
- Increase = 10 on 200 = **5%** ✔

(Isi ka matlab: agle saal ka CI − pichhle saal ka CI = pichhle saal ke CI par ek saal ka interest.)

### Q: 4th year ka CI = ₹6250, rate 8%. 5th year ka CI?
- 8% = 2/25 → multiplier 27/25
- 5th year = 6250 × 27/25 = **₹6750**

### Q: 1st year ka CI = ₹6400, rate 12.5% (1/8). 4th year ka CI?
- Multiplier 9/8, teen baar (1st → 4th)
- 4th year CI = 6400 × (9/8)³ = 6400 × 729/512 = **₹9112.50**

### Q: P = ₹6250, 20%, 4th year ka CI?
- 1st year CI = 20% of 6250 = 1250
- 4th year = 1250 × (6/5)³ = 1250 × 216/125 = **₹2160**

### Q: ₹5000, 10%. 2nd aur 3rd year ke CI me difference?
- Slab: 2nd year interest = 500 + 50 = 550; 3rd year = 500 + 50 + 50 + 5 = 605
- Difference = **₹55**
- Shortcut: 2nd year CI (550) par 10% = **55** ✔

---

## 4.7 Special Types

### 4.7.1 Rate nikalna jab P aur A diye ho
**Steps:**
1. P : A ratio simplify karo.
2. Time ke hisab se **root** lo (2 saal → square root, 3 saal → cube root).
3. Jo ratio mile (jaise 20 : 21), usse **(increase/original) × 100** karo.

**Q:** ₹6400, A = ₹7056 (2 saal). Rate?
- 6400 : 7056 = 400 : 441 (÷16)
- √ → 20 : 21
- Rate = (1/20) × 100 = **5%**

### 4.7.2 Do alag-alag time ka Amount diya ho
**Q:** Koi sum 3 saal me ₹7800, 5 saal me ₹11,232 ho jata hai (CI). Rate?
- 3 → 5 saal = **2 saal ka journey**
- Yahan "7800" ko principal maano, "11232" ko amount, time = 2 yr
- Ratio 7800 : 11232 = **25 : 36**
- √ → **5 : 6** → increase 1 on 5 → **20%**

### 4.7.3 Triple / "n times" type (Time nikalna)
**Concept:** Agar x years me k guna hota hai aur hame y guna dena hai, to **powers ko compare karo (same base)**.

**Q:** Sum 10 years me 3 guna hota hai (CI). Kitne saal me **243 guna** hoga?
- 243 = **3⁵**, 3 = **3¹**
- 1 power ↔ 10 years → 5 power ↔ **10 × 5 = 50 years**

**Rule:** (Known time) × (new power / old power) = New time.

---

## 4.8 Half-yearly aur Quarterly Compounding

| Compounding | Rate | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **Half-yearly** | **R ÷ 2** | **T × 2** |
| **Quarterly** | **R ÷ 4** | **T × 4** |

**Ex:** R = 20% p.a., T = 2 yr, half-yearly → R = 10%, T = 4
**Ex:** R = 20% p.a., T = 2 yr, quarterly → R = 5%, T = 8

Baaki saara kuch (slab/chain/successive) same hi hai.

---

## 4.9 Effective Rate (SI vs CI)

**2 years ke liye:** Effective % = **a + b + (ab/100)** (a = b = rate)

| Rate | SI (2 yr) | CI (2 yr) |
|---|---|---|
| 10% | 10 + 10 = **20%** | 10 + 10 + (100/100) = **21%** |
| 20% | 20 + 20 = **40%** | 20 + 20 + (400/100) = **44%** |

**Use:** ₹6000, 20%, 2 yr CI = 44% of 6000 = **₹2640** (seedha!)

**3 years ke liye:** pehle 2 saal ka effective rate (21%) nikalo, phir usse 1 saal wale rate (10%) ke saath combine karo.
- 21 + 10 + (21 × 10)/100 = 31 + 2.1 = **33.1%**

---

## 4.10 CI − SI ka Difference (Formulas)

**2 years:**

**CI − SI = P × (R/100)²** (jise likhte hain PR²/100²)

**3 years:**

**CI − SI = P × R²(300 + R) / 100³**

*(Ye formulas sirf is type ke "difference" questions ke liye. Time 2 saal ya 3 saal hona chahiye.)*

## 6. Common Mistakes

1. **"Interest" ki definition me sirf "byaaj" likhna** — ye meaning hai, definition nahi. "Extra money to be paid" likho.
2. **R/100 ki jagah R ke fraction me phir se /100 lagana** (jaise 1/7 ko dobara 100 se divide).
3. **Amount aur SI ka time alag hone par subtract karna** (A − SI tabhi jab time same ho).
4. **"Equal amount" aur "equal interest" ko mix karna** — equal amount me Principal ka 100% add karna padta hai.
5. **Question ke last word ko ignore karna** (principal puchha tha, amount nikal diya, ya CI puchha tha, Amount likh diya).
6. **Specific-year CI aur total CI ko mix karna** — "2nd year ka CI" ≠ "2 saal ka CI".
7. **Slab method me pattern bhoolna** — 2 yr = 1:2:1, 3 yr = 1:3:3:1.
8. **Half-yearly/quarterly me sirf rate ya sirf time badalna** — dono badalna padta hai.
9. **Fractional time (2¼ yrs) me poora 3rd saal le lena** — sirf ¼ part lena hai.

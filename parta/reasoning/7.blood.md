# Blood Relations — Complete Notes

> **Topic:** Blood Relations / रक्त संबंध
> **Level:** Beginner → Exam Level
> **Goal:** Understand family relationships, draw family trees, solve coded relations, and answer statement-based questions quickly.

---

# 1. What is Blood Relation?

Blood Relation questions ask:

> **"How is A related to B?"**

or

> **"What is the relation of A with respect to B?"**

Examples:

* Father
* Mother
* Brother
* Sister
* Son
* Daughter
* Uncle
* Aunt
* Grandfather
* Grandmother
* Cousin
* Nephew
* Niece
* Husband
* Wife
* Father-in-law
* Mother-in-law
* Brother-in-law
* Sister-in-law

The main idea is simple:

> **Build the relationship step-by-step instead of trying to guess the answer.**

---

# 2. Most Important Rule — Never Guess Gender from a Name

This is one of the most important points emphasized in the transcript.

## Rule

> **Never predict the gender of a person from their name unless the question explicitly gives the gender.**

For example:

```text
Ravi
```

Do NOT automatically assume:

```text
Ravi = Male
```

unless the question tells us.

Similarly:

```text
Suman
```

could be male or female.

### Why?

Because reasoning questions are based on the information given in the question, not on our personal assumptions.

---

# 3. Three Main Types of Blood Relation Questions

The transcript divides Blood Relation questions into three broad types:

### Type 1 — Statement-Based Questions

A person points toward someone and describes the relationship.

Example:

> A man points towards a girl and says,
> "She is the daughter of my brother."

We have to find the relation between the man and the girl.

---

### Type 2 — Family Tree Questions

A family structure is given and we have to identify relationships.

Example:

```text
Grandfather
    |
   Father
    |
    A
```

Question:

> How is A related to the grandfather?

Answer:

**Grandson / Granddaughter**, depending on A's gender.

---

### Type 3 — Coded Relations

Symbols/operators are used instead of words.

For example, the transcript uses symbols such as:

```text
+
-
×
÷
$
@
```

The symbols represent different relationships according to the question.

The important thing is:

> **Do not blindly memorize the symbol. Read what the question defines the symbol to mean.**

# 5. Paternal Side vs Maternal Side

This is extremely important.

## Paternal = Father's Side

Your father's family:

```text
Father's Father → Grandfather
Father's Mother → Grandmother
Father's Brother → Uncle
Father's Sister → Aunt
```

In Hindi:

```text
Father's Father = दादा
Father's Mother = दादी
Father's Brother = चाचा / ताऊ
Father's Sister = बुआ
```

### Example

> Father's sister

Think:

```text
Father
  |
  Sister
```

Answer:

**Bua / बुआ**

---

# 6. Maternal Side

Maternal = Mother's side.

```text
Mother's Father → Maternal Grandfather
Mother's Mother → Maternal Grandmother
Mother's Brother → Maternal Uncle
Mother's Sister → Maternal Aunt
```

In Hindi:

```text
Mother's Father = नाना
Mother's Mother = नानी
Mother's Brother = मामा
Mother's Sister = मौसी
```

# 7. Easy Family Tree

Think about your family like this:

```text
             Grandfather ─── Grandmother
                    |
          ┌─────────┴─────────┐
       Father               Uncle/Aunt
          |
        YOU
```

Your father's brother's child:

```text
Father
  |
  Brother
     |
   Child
```

That child is your:

**Cousin**

---

# 8. Cousin — Very Important

A cousin is generally:

> **Child of your parent's brother or sister.**

For example:

```text
          Grandparents
               |
       ┌───────┴───────┐
     Father           Uncle
       |                 |
      You              C
```

Here:

**You and C = Cousins**

### Shortcut

To reach your cousin:

> **Go from yourself → parent → parent's brother/sister → their child.**

---

# 9. Nephew and Niece

Your brother's/sister's child:

```text
Brother/Sister
      |
   Child
```

If the child is male:

**Nephew**

Hindi:

**भतीजा / भांजा**

If the child is female:

**Niece**

Hindi:

**भतीजी / भांजी**

---

# 10. Generation Gap

Generation is very useful when drawing a family tree.

### One generation upward

```text
You
 |
Father/Mother
```

You → Parent = **1 generation**

### Two generations upward

```text
You
 |
Father
 |
Grandfather
```

You → Grandfather = **2 generations**

### One generation downward

```text
You
 |
Son/Daughter
```

You → Child = **1 generation down**

# 11. Family Tree Drawing Trick

The transcript recommends using simple lines rather than making complicated diagrams.

A basic structure:

```text
       Father
          |
         YOU
          |
        Son
```

For horizontal relationships:

```text
Father ───── Uncle
```

For husband-wife:

```text
Husband ─── Wife
```

For siblings:

```text
        Parent
          |
      ────┼────
      |   |   |
    A     B   C
```

A, B and C are siblings.

# 12. The Biggest Trick — Move Through the Relationship

Do not try to understand the complete sentence at once.

Break it into small pieces.

Example:

> A is the daughter of B's brother.

Start with:

```text
B
|
Brother
|
Daughter
```

Therefore:

**A = B's niece**

---

# 13. "How is A Related to B?" — Very Important

The direction of the question matters.

Suppose:

```text
Ravi → Father → Son
```

If the question is:

> How is the son related to Ravi?

Answer:

**Son**

But if the question is:

> How is Ravi related to the son?

Answer:

**Father**

So always ask yourself:

> **Whose relation are they asking for?**

---

# 14. "A is Related to B" — Read Carefully

The transcript repeatedly emphasizes understanding the direction.

Example:

> "How is A related to B?"

Means:

> **What is A's relationship with B?**

Not:

> What is B's relationship with A?

### Example

If:

```text
A = Father of B
```

Then:

**A related to B = Father**

But:

**B related to A = Son/Daughter**

The word **ONLY** gives extra information.

The transcript uses this idea in questions such as:

# 21. Husband-Wife Relationship

Marriage creates an important bridge in Blood Relations.

Example:

```text
A ─── Wife
```

If the question says:

> A's wife's father

Move:

```text
A
 |
Wife
 |
Father
```

That person is:

**Father-in-law of A**

# 22. In-Law Relations

"In-law" relationships generally require crossing a married couple.

For example:

```text
A ─── B
```

If A and B are husband and wife:

* A's wife's father = Father-in-law
* A's wife's mother = Mother-in-law
* A's wife's brother = Brother-in-law
* A's wife's sister = Sister-in-law

### Important

The transcript emphasizes:

> **To reach an in-law relation, you generally need to cross the husband-wife relationship.**

# 27. Coded Relation Questions

These are questions where symbols represent relationships.

For example, a question may define something like:

```text
A + B
A - B
A × B
A ÷ B
```

The symbols are not mathematical operations here.

They are simply **codes for relationships**.

---

# 28. Golden Rule for Coded Relations

> **Never treat +, -, ×, ÷ as normal mathematics.**

Instead:

```text
Symbol → Relationship
```

First understand what each symbol means.

Then solve the chain.

---

# 29. Example of Coded Relation

Suppose a question defines:

```text
A + B = A is brother of B
```

Then:

```text
A + B
```

means:

**A is B's brother.**

If:

```text
B × C = B is father of C
```

then:

```text
A + B × C
```

can be understood step-by-step:

```text
A → Brother of B
B → Father of C
```

Therefore A is:

**Uncle of C**

---

# 30. Read Coded Relations Step-by-Step

Never solve the entire expression in your head.

For:

```text
A + B - C × D
```

do:

```text
A + B
↓
Understand first relation

B - C
↓
Understand second relation

C × D
↓
Understand third relation
```

Then connect them.

---

# 31. Important Coded-Relation Trick — First Person

The transcript repeatedly emphasizes identifying the **first person**.

When you see something like:

```text
A related to B
```

ask:

> **Who is A?**

The answer should describe A's relationship with B.

This avoids reversing the answer.

---

# 32. Forward Type

The transcript refers to some coded questions as **forward type**.

In these, the relationship is followed in the given direction.

Example:

```text
A → B → C → D
```

Start from A and move toward D.

The key is:

> **Follow the relation in the direction given by the question.**

---

# 33. Backward Type

Some questions ask for the reverse relationship.

For example:

```text
A → B → C
```

But the question may ask:

> What is A related to C?

or

> What is C related to A?

These are different.

### Trick

Before solving:

**Circle/identify the two people being asked about.**

Then trace between them.

---

# 34. Do Not Draw More Than Necessary

One of the transcript's practical tricks is:

> **Make only the diagram you need.**

If the question only requires:

```text
A → B → C
```

don't draw the entire extended family.

This saves:

* Time
* Effort
* Confusion

---

# 35. Bottom-to-Top Trick

The transcript emphasizes a useful strategy:

> **When solving family-tree relationships, move upward when possible.**

Why?

Because parents, grandparents, etc. are already established above you.

For example:

```text
        Grandfather
             |
          Father
             |
            YOU
```

If asked about Grandfather, simply move:

```text
YOU
 ↑
Father
 ↑
Grandfather
```

---

# 36. Gender-Based Shortcut

The transcript gives another useful way of thinking.

When you move from a **male person**, think in terms of the male relationship.

When you move from a **female person**, think in terms of the female relationship.

But:

> **Only use gender that is actually established by the question.**

Never guess gender from a name.

### Step 2

### Step 3

### Step 5

# 73. Coded Relations — Important Concept

Suppose a question says:

```text
A + B means A is brother of B
A @ B means A is sister of B
A $ B means A is mother of B
A - B means A is father of B
```

Then:

```text
A + B
```

means:

**A is B's brother.**

And:

```text
A $ B
```

means:

**A is B's mother.**

---

# 74. Coded Relation Example

Given:

```text
A + B
B - C
```

and:

```text
+ = brother
- = father
```

Then:

```text
A = brother of B
B = father of C
```

Therefore:

**A = Uncle of C**

---

# 75. Coded Relation — Don't Use Mathematics

If you see:

```text
A + B × C
```

DO NOT calculate:

```text
A + B × C
```

as mathematics.

Instead read:

```text
A is ___ of B
B is ___ of C
```

It is a family relationship problem.

---

# 76. Coded Relation — Read the Definition First

If the question says:

```text
P + Q means P is the brother of Q.
P - Q means P is the father of Q.
```

Then write these meanings separately before solving.

This prevents confusion.

then the reverse is:

**B is sibling of A**

More specifically, because A is male:

**B is A's sibling**.

The exact gender of B may need additional information.

* There are 3+ relationships.
* Marriage is involved.
* There are multiple siblings.

# 120. Common Mistakes

## Mistake 1 — Reversing the relationship

```text
A = Father of B
```

Wrong:

> B is father of A.

Correct:

> A is father of B.

---

## Mistake 2 — Guessing gender

Don't assume:

```text
Name = Male/Female
```

unless the question tells you.

---

## Mistake 3 — Ignoring "only"

```text
Only son
Only daughter
Only brother
Only sister
```

These words can remove ambiguity.

---

## Mistake 4 — Getting confused by names

Ignore names initially.

Think:

```text
Father → Brother → Daughter
```

instead of:

```text
Ramesh → Suresh → Kavita
```

---

## Mistake 5 — Drawing too much

Don't create a huge family tree when only 3 people are required.

---

## Mistake 6 — Forgetting marriage

For:

```text
wife's father
husband's mother
wife's brother
husband's sister
```

remember that you must cross the husband-wife relationship.

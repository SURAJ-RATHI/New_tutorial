# 🪑 Seating Arrangement — Complete Notes

> **Goal:** Learn how to solve Seating Arrangement questions quickly and accurately using diagrams, fixed positions, direction rules, and shortcut methods.

---

# 1. What is Seating Arrangement?

In Seating Arrangement questions, some people are sitting in a particular arrangement and you are given clues about their positions.

You have to find things like:

* Who sits to the left/right of someone?
* Who sits opposite someone?
* Who is the immediate neighbour?
* Who sits between two people?
* What is the position of a person?
* Who is sitting at the centre?
* Which arrangement is possible/impossible?

The basic idea is:

```text
Given statements
       ↓
Make arrangement
       ↓
Apply all conditions
       ↓
Find the required answer
```

---

# 2. Most Important Rule

## Don't try to solve everything mentally.

Make a small diagram.

For example:

```text
A   B   C   D   E   F
```

Then place the people according to the clues.

The lecture repeatedly emphasizes that proper arrangement makes even difficult questions manageable.

---

# 3. Types of Seating Arrangement

The major types are:

### Type 1 — Linear Arrangement

People sit in a straight line.

```text
A   B   C   D   E   F
```

---

### Type 2 — Circular Arrangement

People sit around a circle.

```text
       A
   F       B

   E       C
       D
```

---

### Type 3 — Circular Arrangement with Different Directions

Some people may face:

* Centre
* Outside

Example:

```text
        A →
    F       B ←

    E       C →

        D ←
```

This is more difficult because everyone's left/right may be different.

---

### Type 4 — Square / Rectangular Arrangement

People sit around a square or rectangle.

```text
A ---- B ---- C
|             |
D             E
|             |
F ---- G ---- H
```

---

# 4. Linear Seating Arrangement

Suppose six people sit in a row:

```text
1   2   3   4   5   6
```

If:

```text
A sits at position 3
```

then:

```text
1   2   A   4   5   6
```

---

# 5. Left and Right in a Straight Line

This is one of the easiest cases.

If everyone is facing **North**:

```text
        NORTH ↑

A   B   C   D   E

LEFT ←       → RIGHT
```

So:

* B is immediately right of A.
* C is second right of A.
* D is third right of A.

Example:

> C is second to the right of A.

```text
A   _   C
```

Because:

```text
A → 1st → 2nd → C
```

---

# 6. If People Face South

This is a common trap.

If everyone faces South:

```text
        SOUTH ↓

E   D   C   B   A
```

Your left/right becomes reversed from your normal viewpoint.

### Remember:

| Facing | Their Left | Their Right |
| ------ | ---------- | ----------- |
| North  | ←          | →           |
| South  | →          | ←           |

### Trick:

> **Facing South = Reverse Left and Right**

---

# 7. Immediate Left / Immediate Right

**Immediate** means exactly next to the person.

Example:

> B is immediately right of A.

```text
A   B
```

There is nobody between A and B.

---

# 8. Second / Third / Fourth to the Left or Right

Suppose:

```text
A   _   _   B
```

B is:

```text
3rd to the right of A
```

because:

```text
A
 ↓
1st
 ↓
2nd
 ↓
3rd
 B
```

---

# 9. "One Person Between"

This is extremely important.

> A and B have one person between them.

Means:

```text
A   X   B
```

There is exactly **one person** between A and B.

Therefore their position difference is:

```text
2
```

---

# 10. Two People Between

> A and B have two people between them.

Means:

```text
A   X   X   B
```

Position difference:

```text
3
```

### Shortcut

If there are **N people between two people**:

```text
Position gap = N + 1
```

Examples:

| People between | Position difference |
| -------------: | ------------------: |
|              0 |                   1 |
|              1 |                   2 |
|              2 |                   3 |
|              3 |                   4 |
|              4 |                   5 |

---

# 11. "Between" vs "Immediate Between"

### Immediate neighbours

```text
A   B   C
```

B is between A and C.

### One person between A and C

```text
A   B   C
```

Again B is the one person between them.

Always carefully read whether the question says:

* immediately between
* one person between
* two persons between
* exactly one person between

---

# 12. Very Important Shortcut from the Lecture

Suppose there are 8 people.

If the question says:

> G and H have one person between them.

Then don't randomly try every position.

You can immediately look for positions having exactly one gap:

```text
1 _ 3
2 _ 4
3 _ 5
4 _ 6
5 _ 7
6 _ 8
```

This can drastically reduce your cases.

---

# 13. Position from an End

Suppose there are 8 people:

```text
1  2  3  4  5  6  7  8
```

If someone is:

> 2nd from the left

then:

```text
_  A  _  _  _  _  _  _
```

If someone is:

> 2nd from the right

then:

```text
_  _  _  _  _  _  A  _
```

---

# 14. Combining Position + Gap

This is an important exam trick from the lecture.

Suppose:

* There are 8 people.
* A has 8 people to his left / A is at an end.
* There are 5 people between A and Z.

Then:

```text
A _ _ _ _ _ Z _ 
```

or the reverse depending on direction.

Instead of trying many arrangements, count the number of occupied positions.

### Remember:

If 5 people are between A and Z:

```text
A + 5 people + Z
```

Total positions occupied:

```text
1 + 5 + 1 = 7
```

So in an 8-person arrangement, only one position remains.

This kind of counting can immediately eliminate impossible cases.

---

# 15. Circular Seating Arrangement

Now people sit in a circle.

Example:

```text
          A
      F       B
    E           C
          D
```

There is no actual "first seat" in a circle.

Therefore:

## Fix one person.

For example:

```text
          A
```

Now arrange everyone else relative to A.

---

# 16. Why Do We Fix One Person?

Suppose six people are sitting around a circle:

```text
A B C D E F
```

The entire circle can rotate.

This:

```text
A B C D E F
```

and this:

```text
C D E F A B
```

represent the same relative arrangement.

So:

> **Fix one person to remove unnecessary rotations.**

---

# 17. Clockwise and Anticlockwise

### Clockwise

Movement like:

```text
↻
```

### Anticlockwise

Movement like:

```text
↺
```

The transcript uses terms such as:

> clockwise direction / दक्षिणावर्त direction

and

> anticlockwise direction.

---

# 18. "One Person Between A and B Clockwise"

Suppose:

> Only one person sits between A and B in clockwise direction.

Then:

```text
A → X → B
```

There is exactly one person between them.

The lecture recommends not wasting time naming every temporary seat.

You can simply label positions:

```text
1  2  3  4  5
```

instead of:

```text
O1 O2 O3 O4 O5
```

This saves time.

---

# 19. Circular Arrangement — Facing Centre

This is one of the **most important rules**.

When everyone faces the centre:

```text
          A
       ↙     ↘

    F             B

    E             C
       ↖       ↗
          D
```

For a person facing the centre:

> **Left = Clockwise**

> **Right = Anticlockwise**

### Memorize:

```text
Facing Centre:

LEFT  → CLOCKWISE
RIGHT → ANTICLOCKWISE
```

---

# 20. Circular Arrangement — Facing Outside

When everyone faces outside:

```text
Facing outside
```

Left/right becomes reversed.

### Memorize:

```text
Facing Outside:

LEFT  → ANTICLOCKWISE
RIGHT → CLOCKWISE
```

So:

| Direction      | Left          | Right         |
| -------------- | ------------- | ------------- |
| Facing Centre  | Clockwise     | Anticlockwise |
| Facing Outside | Anticlockwise | Clockwise     |

---

# 21. Easy Memory Trick

Remember:

## Centre → L = Clockwise

## Outside → L = Anticlockwise

Or:

```text
CENTRE  → LEFT = CLOCKWISE
OUTSIDE → LEFT = ANTICLOCKWISE
```

---

# 22. Mixed Facing Arrangement

This is the difficult type highlighted in the transcript.

Some people face:

```text
CENTRE
```

while others face:

```text
OUTSIDE
```

Example:

```text
A → Centre
B → Outside
C → Centre
D → Outside
```

Now you **cannot use one common left/right rule for everyone**.

You must check the direction of the particular person mentioned in the statement.

---

# 23. Most Important Rule for Mixed Facing

Suppose:

> O faces outside.

and

> Q is third to the right of O.

You must calculate **O's right**, not your right.

Since O faces outside:

```text
O's RIGHT = clockwise
```

Then count three positions clockwise.

---

# 24. Example — Mixed Facing

Suppose O faces outside.

Question:

> Q is third to the right of O.

Start from O:

```text
O → 1st → 2nd → 3rd → Q
```

But the direction must be determined from **O's facing direction**.

Do not use the direction of another person.

---

# 25. Individual Direction Matters

This is a major source of mistakes.

Suppose:

```text
N faces Centre
O faces Outside
```

If the statement says:

> O is third to the right of N

Use **N's direction**, because O's position is being described relative to N.

If the statement says:

> N is third to the left of O

Use **O's direction**.

### Golden Rule:

> **Always check whose left/right is being used.**

---

# 26. Opposite Position

Opposite seating is generally associated with an **even number of people**.

Example: 6 people.

```text
          A
      F       B

      E       C
          D
```

Opposite pairs:

```text
A ↔ D
B ↔ E
C ↔ F
```

---

# 27. Opposite Shortcut

For:

```text
6 people
```

opposite means:

```text
3 seats away
```

For:

```text
8 people
```

opposite means:

```text
4 seats away
```

Generally:

```text
Opposite distance = N / 2
```

where N = total number of people.

---

# 28. Fourth Left / Fourth Right

A useful shortcut discussed in the transcript:

With **8 people**, the person who is 4th left/right is opposite.

```text
8 / 2 = 4
```

So:

> 4th left = opposite

and

> 4th right = opposite

---

# 29. 10 People Case

With 10 people:

```text
10 / 2 = 5
```

Therefore:

> 5th left/right = opposite.

### Shortcut

```text
8 people  → 4th = opposite
10 people → 5th = opposite
12 people → 6th = opposite
```

---

# 30. Immediate Neighbour

Immediate neighbour means directly adjacent.

For example:

```text
A B C
```

B is the immediate neighbour of both A and C.

In a circle, each person normally has:

```text
2 immediate neighbours
```

One on each side.

---

# 31. "A is the Neighbour of B"

This only tells us:

```text
A — B
```

A can be on either side unless another clue specifies left/right.

Do not assume:

```text
A B
```

instead of:

```text
B A
```

unless required.

---

# 32. Very Important Exam Trick — Use Strong Clues First

When solving an arrangement, don't necessarily follow the statements in the order given.

Start with the strongest information.

### Strong clues:

1. Fixed position
2. Centre position
3. Opposite position
4. Exact number of people between
5. Immediate left/right
6. Second/third/fourth position
7. Neighbour information

---

# 33. Example of Strong-Clue Strategy

Suppose:

* A sits opposite B.
* C is immediately left of A.
* D is neighbour of B.
* E is second right of C.

Start with:

```text
A ↔ B
```

Then place:

```text
C
```

Then:

```text
D
```

Then:

```text
E
```

This is much faster than trying to place A, B, C, D, E randomly.

---

# 34. Case Method

Sometimes more than one arrangement is possible.

Don't panic.

Make:

```text
Case 1
Case 2
```

Then test every clue.

Example:

```text
Case 1: A B C D E F
Case 2: A F E D C B
```

Check which one satisfies all conditions.

---

# 35. Eliminate Cases

Suppose you create two possibilities:

```text
Case 1 ✓
Case 2 ✗
```

If one violates even a single statement:

> Remove that case.

You do not need to solve every case completely.

---

# 36. Use "Hold" Positions

The lecture demonstrates a useful approach:

If you don't yet know the exact position, keep the relationship in mind instead of forcing a placement.

Example:

> A and B have one person between them clockwise.

Write:

```text
A _ B
```

and keep it pending.

Later, when another clue gives a fixed position, place them.

This avoids unnecessary cases.

---

# 37. Don't Waste Time Naming Every Seat

Suppose there are five unknown positions.

Instead of:

```text
O1 O2 O3 O4 O5
```

you can simply use:

```text
1 2 3 4 5
```

The lecture specifically recommends simple numbering to save exam time.

---

# 38. Mixed Direction — Advanced Example

Suppose:

* N faces inward.
* O faces outward.
* O is third right of N.

First identify:

```text
N → inward
```

For N:

```text
Right = anticlockwise
```

Then count:

```text
1 → 2 → 3
```

and place O there.

Do **not** use O's direction to determine this relation.

---

# 39. If the Statement Says "O's Immediate Left"

Now the reference person is O.

So check:

```text
O faces what direction?
```

If O faces outside:

```text
O's Left = anticlockwise
```

Then place the required person there.

---

### Never assume everyone faces the same direction.

If the question explicitly says:

```text
Some face centre.
```

# 42. Square Arrangement

Sometimes people sit around a square.

Example:

```text
A -------- B
|          |
|          |
D -------- C
```

The important concepts are:

* Corner positions
* Side positions
* Opposite positions
* Clockwise direction
* Anticlockwise direction

---

# 43. Rectangle Arrangement

For a rectangle:

```text
A ---- B ---- C
|             |
D             E
|             |
F ---- G ---- H
```

Again, think of it as a circular path around the rectangle.

Clockwise:

```text
A → B → C → E → H → G → F → D → A
```

---

# 44. Corner vs Side

In square/rectangular arrangements, carefully identify whether the question refers to:

* corner
* side
* opposite corner
* adjacent position

Don't treat every seat as if it were in a straight line.

---

# 45. Centre Position of a Line

The transcript also discusses questions involving a person being at the **centre position of a line**.

For an odd number of positions:

```text
1 2 3 4 5
```

the centre is:

```text
3
```

For:

```text
7 positions
```

centre:

```text
4
```

Formula:

```text
Centre = (N + 1) / 2
```

for odd N.

---

# 46. Two Lines / Multiple Lines

Some exam questions can involve more than one line.

Example:

```text
Line 1: A B C D E
Line 2: F G H I J
```

If the question says someone is in the centre of Line 2:

```text
F G H I J
    ↑
    H
```

H is the centre.

Always identify which line the question is referring to.

---

# 47. "Second Right of O"

Suppose:

```text
A B C D E F
```

If O is at B:

```text
B → C → D
```

Therefore:

```text
D = second right of O
```

In circular questions, apply the person's facing direction before counting.

---

# 48. "Third Left"

If:

```text
A _ _ B
```

B is third left/right depending on the reference person's facing direction.

Always count:

```text
1
2
3
```

Don't accidentally count the reference person as 1.

---

# 49. Common Counting Mistake

Wrong:

```text
A = 1
B = 2
C = 3
```

when asked:

> third to the right of A.

Correct:

```text
A
↓
1st = B
2nd = C
3rd = D
```

So:

```text
D
```

is third right of A.

---

# 50. "Between Two People" — Important Interpretation

If the question asks:

> Who sits between G and H?

and there is exactly one person between them:

```text
G   X   H
```

Answer:

```text
X
```

The transcript highlights this type of question as appearing in recent exams.

---

# 51. Target-Based Solving

Sometimes you don't need to construct the entire arrangement.

Suppose the question asks:

> Who sits between G and H?

If the clues force:

```text
G _ H
```

then you only need to determine that middle position.

You can stop once the required information is confirmed.

---

# 52. Option-Based Solving

Sometimes options can help.

Suppose the question asks:

> Who is second to the right of H?

and the options are:

```text
A
B
C
D
```

If you already know the possible locations of H, you can use the options to eliminate possibilities.

The lecture demonstrates using option/reference positions when an arrangement has a limited number of possible cases.

---

# 53. Do Not Over-Solve

A very important exam lesson:

> You don't always need the complete arrangement.

If the question asks:

```text
Who is immediate right of E?
```

and you can determine E's immediate right directly:

**Stop there.**

Don't spend extra time arranging everyone else.

---

Use this workflow:

```text
STEP 1
Read the complete question.

        ↓

STEP 2

        ↓

STEP 3
Identify arrangement type.

Linear?
Circular?
Square?
Rectangle?

        ↓

STEP 4
Check facing direction.

Centre?
Outside?

        ↓

STEP 5
Find strongest clue.

Fixed position?

        ↓
```

# 56. Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1 — Reversing Left and Right

Especially in circular arrangements.

Remember:

```text
Centre:
Left → Clockwise
Right → Anticlockwise
```

```text
Outside:
Left → Anticlockwise
Right → Clockwise
```

---

### Mistake 2 — Counting the Reference Person

Wrong:

```text
A = first
```

Correct:

```text
Person next to A = first
```

---

### Mistake 3 — Forgetting Direction

If:

```text
A faces centre
B faces outside
```

their left/right are different.

---

### Mistake 4 — Assuming Everyone Faces Centre

Never do this unless the question says so.

---

### Mistake 5 — Starting With a Weak Clue

Don't begin with:

> A is a neighbour of B.

Start with stronger information such as:

> A is opposite B.

---

### Mistake 6 — Making Too Many Cases

First use counting and fixed-position information to eliminate impossible cases.

---

### Mistake 7 — Solving More Than Required

If the question asks one relationship, don't waste time finding the complete arrangement unless necessary.

# 60. Mini Practice Questions

## Question 1

Six people A, B, C, D, E and F sit in a line facing North.

B is immediately right of A.

C is second right of B.

Who is third right of A?

### Answer

```text
A B C _ _ _
```

C is second right of A.

Third right would be the next position.

---

## Question 2

Eight people sit in a circle.

Who sits opposite A?

### Answer

The person **4 positions away** from A.

Because:

```text
8 / 2 = 4
```

---

## Question 3

Six people sit in a circle facing the centre.

B is immediately left of A.

Which direction should you move from A?

### Answer

For someone facing centre:

```text
Left = Clockwise
```

Therefore move **clockwise**.

---

## Question 4

Eight people sit in a circle.

A is facing outside.

B is second right of A.

Which direction should you count?

### Answer

Facing outside:

```text
Right = Clockwise
```

So count two positions clockwise.

---

## Question 5

A and B have two people between them.

Write the basic pattern.

### Answer

```text
A X X B
```

---

## Question 6

There are 10 people in a circle.

Which position is opposite A?

### Answer

```text
10 / 2 = 5
```

Therefore:

```text
5th position
```

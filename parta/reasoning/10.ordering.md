# Order & Ranking — Complete Notes

## 1. What is Order & Ranking?

**Order & Ranking** means finding the position/rank of a person or object in a line, row, class, race, etc.

Example:

```text
A  B  C  D  E  F  G  H  I  J
```

If **E** is:

* 5th from the left
* 6th from the right

then total persons are:

$$
5+6-1=10
$$

Why `-1`?

Because **E is counted twice**:

* once while counting from the left
* once while counting from the right

So we subtract it once.

---

# 2. Golden Formula

## Total = Left + Right − 1

If a person's position is known from both ends:

$$
\boxed{T=L+R-1}
$$

Where:

* `T` = Total number
* `L` = Position from left/start/top/etc.
* `R` = Position from right/end/bottom/etc.

### Example

Aman is 14th from the left and 9th from the right.

$$
14+9-1=22
$$

**Answer = 22**

---

# 3. The Two Ends Don't Have to Be Left & Right

The same concept works whenever the question gives positions from **two opposite ends**.

They can be:

| First End | Second End |
| --------- | ---------- |
| Left      | Right      |
| Top       | Bottom     |
| Start     | Last       |
| Before    | After      |
| Ahead     | Behind     |

The basic idea remains:

$$
\boxed{\text{Total}=\text{Position from End 1}+\text{Position from End 2}-1}
$$

### Example

Meena is:

* 8th from the top
* 7th from the bottom

Then:

$$
8+7-1=14
$$

Total = **14**

---

# 4. If Total is Given

The same formula can be rearranged.

$$
T=L+R-1
$$

Therefore:

### Find Right

$$
\boxed{R=T-L+1}
$$

### Find Left

$$
\boxed{L=T-R+1}
$$

### Example

There are 30 students.

Priyanka is 23rd from the left.

Find her position from the right.

$$
R=30-23+1
$$

$$
R=8
$$

**Answer = 8th from the right**

---

# 5. Very Important Shortcut

If **Total is given**, remember:

> **The sum of the two end positions is always one more than the total.**

Therefore:

$$
\boxed{L+R=T+1}
$$

### Example

Total = 28

Then:

$$
L+R=29
$$

If one side is 6:

$$
6+R=29
$$

$$
R=23
$$

So the other position is **23**.

---

# 6. Three Basic Cases

## Case 1 — Left + Right Given

Find total:

$$
T=L+R-1
$$

### Example

14th from left, 9th from right:

$$
14+9-1=22
$$

---

## Case 2 — Total + Left Given

Find right:

$$
R=T-L+1
$$

### Example

Total = 30, left = 23

$$
R=30-23+1=8
$$

---

## Case 3 — Total + Right Given

Find left:

$$
L=T-R+1
$$

### Example

Total = 40, right = 16

$$
L=40-16+1=25
$$

---

# 7. Position of a Person — Don't Count the Person Twice

Suppose someone is 15th in a race.

Question:

**How many people are ahead of him?**

Don't answer 15.

The person himself occupies the 15th position.

Therefore:

$$
15-1=14
$$

**Answer = 14 people**

### Simple Rule

If a person is in position `N`:

* People before him = `N − 1`
* People after him = `Total − N`

---

# 8. Example — Race

There are 40 runners.

Anu is 15th.

How many runners are ahead of Anu?

$$
15-1=14
$$

**Answer = 14**

The total number of runners is irrelevant for this question.

---

# 9. Be Careful About Gender-Specific Questions

This is a very important trap.

Suppose:

> Sachin is 10th from the left and 15th from the right. How many **boys** are in the row?

First calculate total:

$$
10+15-1=24
$$

So the **total number of people = 24**.

But can we say there are 24 boys?

**No.**

Why?

Because the question never says the row contains only boys.

There could be boys + girls.

Therefore:

> Number of boys **cannot be determined**.

### If the question said:

> In a row of boys, Sachin is 10th from the left and 15th from the right.

Then:

$$
10+15-1=24
$$

So boys = **24**.

### Golden Rule

> Don't confuse **total people** with **total boys/girls** unless the question clearly tells you the row contains only that group.

---

# 10. Example — Students and Boys/Girls

There are **96 students**.

Boys are `1/3` of the total.

### Boys

$$
96\times\frac13=32
$$

### Girls

$$
96-32=64
$$

So:

* Boys = **32**
* Girls = **64**

---

# 11. Ranking + Gender Questions

Suppose:

* Total students = 96
* Boys = 32
* Girls = 64
* Meena is 12th from the top
* 4 boys are ahead of Meena
* Find girls behind Meena.

Among the first 12 positions:

* Boys = 4
* Therefore girls =

$$
12-4=8
$$

Those 8 girls **include Meena herself**, because Meena is one of the first 12 students.

Total girls = 64.

Girls behind Meena:

$$
64-8=56
$$

**Answer = 56**

### Important

If the question asks for the people **behind**, first find how many of that group are already in front/including the current position, then subtract from the total group.

---

# 12. Equal Boys and Girls

Example:

There are 96 students.

Boys and girls are equal.

Therefore:

$$
96\div2=48
$$

So:

* Boys = 48
* Girls = 48

Suppose Ritu is 20th from the top and **13 boys are ahead of her**.

Among the first 20:

$$
20-13=7
$$

So there are 7 girls in the first 20 positions.

These 7 include Ritu.

Total girls = 48.

Girls behind Ritu:

$$
48-7=41
$$

**Answer = 41**

---

# 13. A Very Important Trap — Insufficient Data

Suppose:

> Arjun is 14th from the left. Raj is 18th from the right. Find the total number of people in the row.

You may think:

$$
14+18-1=31
$$

But this is **not necessarily correct**.

Why?

Because Arjun and Raj are **different people**.

We don't know how many people are between them.

Example:

```text
Left
↓
A ................ R
```

The gap between A and R is unknown.

Therefore, total cannot be determined.

### Correct Answer

$$
\boxed{\text{Data is not sufficient}}
$$

or

$$
\boxed{\text{Cannot be determined}}
$$

---

# 14. Why the Normal Formula Doesn't Work Here

The formula:

$$
L+R-1
$$

works directly when **both positions belong to the same person**.

Example:

> A is 14th from left and 9th from right.

Correct:

$$
14+9-1=22
$$

But:

> A is 14th from left and B is 18th from right.

You cannot directly add them because A and B are different people.

You need information about the number of people between them.

---

# 15. Six Major Types of Order & Ranking Questions

The lecture identifies mainly **six types**:

### 1. Total

Find total number of people.

### 2. Position of Person

Find someone's position from left/right/top/bottom.

### 3. Interchanging

Two people exchange positions.

### 4. Maximum

Find maximum possible number of people.

### 5. Minimum

Find minimum possible number of people.

### 6. Middle

Find number of people between two persons.

The lecture especially emphasizes **Middle** questions as an important current exam pattern.

---

# 16. Interchanging — What Does It Mean?

**Interchanging means two people exchange their positions.**

Example:

Before:

```text
A        B
```

After exchanging:

```text
B        A
```

Everyone else stays in the same position.

---

# 17. Interchange Example

Suppose:

* Abhishek = 18th from left
* Aman = 12th from right

They exchange positions.

After exchanging:

* Aman takes Abhishek's old position.
* Abhishek takes Aman's old position.

Suppose after the exchange, Aman becomes **20th from the right**.

We need total people.

After the exchange:

* Aman is 20th from right.
* Aman is now sitting at Abhishek's **old position**.
* Abhishek was 18th from left.

Therefore:

$$
T=18+20-1
$$

$$
T=37
$$

**Answer = 37**

---

# 18. Interchange Shortcut

This is one of the most useful tricks from the lecture.

When two people exchange places and the question gives the **new position of one person**:

> Take the **old position of the other person**, add the new position, and subtract 1.

### Formula

$$
\boxed{T=\text{New position of A}+\text{Old position of B}-1}
$$

where A and B exchanged places.

### Example

* Durgesh = 14th from right
* Deepak = 17th from left
* They exchange places
* After exchange, Deepak = 25th from left

Since Deepak now occupies Durgesh's old position:

$$
14+25-1=38
$$

**Answer = 38**

---

# 19. Interchange Shortcut — Remember This

### Before exchange

```text
A → Position X
B → Position Y
```

### After exchange

```text
B → Position X
A → Position Y
```

If B's new position is given, combine it with **A's old position**.

Don't accidentally use B's old position.

---

# 20. Another Interchange Example

Suppose:

* Mohit = 16th from one side
* Raghav = another position
* They exchange positions
* After exchange, Raghav = 20th from the opposite side.

Then:

$$
16+20-1=35
$$

**Answer = 35**

The important thing is not memorizing the names.

Remember the logic:

> **After exchange, use the new position of one person + old position of the other person − 1.**

# 22. Important Question: Descending Order

Suppose:

> There are 54 students. Sachin is 20th from the top. If students are arranged in descending order, what is Sachin's position from the bottom?

Use:

$$
T=L+R-1
$$

Therefore:

$$
R=T-L+1
$$

$$
R=54-20+1
$$

$$
R=35
$$

**Answer = 35th from bottom**

### Shortcut

For total `T` and position `P`:

$$
\boxed{\text{Opposite position}=T-P+1}
$$

# 23. Order & Ranking Formula Sheet

| Situation                  | Formula                                          |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Total from both ends       | `L + R - 1`                                      |
| Right from total & left    | `T - L + 1`                                      |
| Left from total & right    | `T - R + 1`                                      |
| People ahead of position P | `P - 1`                                          |
| People behind              | `T - P`                                          |
| Sum of opposite positions  | `T + 1`                                          |
| Interchange total          | `New position + Other person's old position - 1` |

# 24. The `+1` and `−1` Logic

This is extremely important.

### When finding total:

$$
\boxed{L+R-1}
$$

Why `-1`?

Because the same person is counted twice.

---

### When finding opposite position:

$$
\boxed{T-P+1}
$$

Why `+1`?

Because the person himself must be included when converting one-end position into the other-end position.

Example:

```text
1  2  3  4  5
↑        ↑
left    right
```

If total = 5 and a person is 2nd from left:

$$
5-2+1=4
$$

So the person is 4th from right.

---

# 25. Common Mistakes

## Mistake 1 — Forgetting `−1`

Wrong:

$$
14+9=23
$$

Correct:

$$
14+9-1=22
$$

---

## Mistake 2 — Forgetting `+1`

Wrong:

$$
30-23=7
$$

Correct:

$$
30-23+1=8
$$

---

## Mistake 3 — Applying the formula to two different people

A = 14th from left
B = 18th from right

Don't automatically do:

$$
14+18-1
$$

The number of people between them is unknown.

---

## Mistake 4 — Confusing total people with total boys/girls

If total row = 24, that does **not** mean boys = 24.

The question must say the row contains only boys.

---

## Mistake 5 — Counting yourself when asking "ahead"

If you are 15th:

People ahead:

$$
15-1=14
$$

Not 15.

---

## Mistake 6 — In interchange questions, using the wrong person's old position

Always ask:

> **Whose new position is given?**

Then use:

> **The other person's old position.**

---

# 26. Fast Exam Strategy

When you see an Order & Ranking question:

### Step 1 — Identify what is being asked

Is it:

* Total?
* Left/right position?
* Ahead/behind?
* Interchange?
* Boys/girls?
* Between/middle?
* Maximum/minimum?

### Step 2 — Identify whether positions belong to the same person

If same person:

$$
L+R-1
$$

may work.

If different people:

**Don't apply it blindly.**

### Step 3 — Check the wording

Look carefully for:

* only boys
* only girls
* students
* people
* children
* runners
* between
* ahead
* behind
* after interchange

### Step 4 — Solve mentally where possible

Most basic Order & Ranking questions are designed for quick calculation.
